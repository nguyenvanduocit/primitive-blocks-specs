# Mobile games hook players with stacked reasons to return

Mobile games keep players by doing three things in order: prove the game is fun within the first few minutes, then give the player a reason to come back at three different time scales (today, this week, this season), then sell speed and convenience to the small minority who pay. The "same pattern in every game" you notice is real, and it has four causes: the economics of buying players (an install costs real money and must be repaid by a few payers over weeks), a short list of well-studied human tendencies, the way ad platforms reward whatever ad gets the most installs, and plain imitation of whatever the top-grossing games ship. The universal must-haves are a short loop with instant feedback, a fast guaranteed early win, a visible next goal, and at least one reason to return tomorrow; almost everything else (battle passes, albums, clans, gacha, prestige, lives) is either an expected feature of top-grossing games or specific to one genre. Marketing is a separate machine that buys the installs; it only pays off if the game then retains and monetizes those installs, which is why "hooking" and "monetizing" are one system measured at different times. The evidence is strong on what games do and weak on how much each feature adds: there is no causal number for any single hook, no reliable 2023-2026 genre-level retention table, no free payer-conversion benchmark, and the popular claim that guild members retain several times better has no traceable source. Regulation (as of 2026-10-22) is tightening around hidden prices, hidden odds and children, not around the mechanics themselves.

*How to read this report.* Every claim carries a source link and a confidence label. HIGH means the source states it directly and it is consistent across sources; MEDIUM means one reasonable source, a vendor with a stated basis, or a secondary summary; LOW means a vendor blog, an old source, a community estimate, or a figure that could not be traced. Where the underlying notes gave a range (for example "LOW to MEDIUM") this report uses the lower label. Jargon is defined on first use. Figures the notes could not trace to any source are left out on purpose.

## Players decide in minutes, so the first session carries the whole game

Every mobile game is built around a **core loop**, the short action, reward, progress cycle the player repeats every few seconds to minutes; one industry guide calls it "the very heartbeat" of the game ([Homa Games](https://www.homagames.com/blog/what-is-a-core-loop-in-a-mobile-game); HIGH for the definition, MEDIUM as a general taxonomy because it is a vendor blog). Wrapped around it is the **meta layer**, the longer-term goals (upgrades, collections, map progress, events) that give a reason to return for days or weeks. One 2026 guide says that by day 3 to 5 the meta layer should be the main reason a player relaunches the app ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/); MEDIUM, read as a search snippet). Terminology is loose: the notes found no authoritative definition of a separate "session loop", so treat that phrase as unstandardized. Across genres the minimal viable stack that fits every source is four layers: an action with instant feedback, a reward on every loop, a visible next goal outside the loop (level, upgrade, unlock), and a reason to return later (timer, event, daily reward). That stack is the notes' own synthesis, not a quoted standard. Hyper-casual games (tiny arcade games monetized mostly by ads) have only the first one or two layers; **hybrid-casual**, defined by Voodoo as a hyper-casual core plus a simplified casual meta layer, adds the rest ([PocketGamer.biz interview](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/77247/corentin-selz-voodoo-from-hypercasual-to-hybrid-casual/); MEDIUM, page not read). Sensor Tower reports that hybrid-casual leaders overtook hypercasual leaders on day-1 retention in 2025 and that the gap widens by day 7 ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-state-of-the-mobile); HIGH).

The **first-time user experience (FTUE)** is the first minutes: tutorial, first rewards, first requests. Consensus advice is to show why the game is fun within two to three minutes, teach by doing, guarantee an early success, and delay everything that feels like friction: paywalls, ads, forced account creation, permission pop-ups. GameAnalytics also recommends gifting premium currency that buys a meaningful upgrade, because it both rewards the player and teaches how buying works ([GameAnalytics](https://gameanalytics.com/blog/tips-for-a-great-first-time-user-experience-ftue-in-f2p-games); HIGH that this is the advice, MEDIUM on the two-to-three-minute figure, which is not backed by data). Roblox's design docs say players "typically decide their interest in a game within minutes", recommend low early XP thresholds so players level up quickly, and ask designers to end onboarding with short-, mid- and long-term goals ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding); MEDIUM, platform-specific). Supersonic describes one game where jumping too quickly between mechanics made 60% of players churn in the tutorial ([Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/optimizing-ftue/); LOW as a benchmark, one case). The permission asks follow a pattern: do not request push notifications on first launch, show a pre-permission explanation first, and ask when the benefit is obvious (for a timer game, the first time a timer starts) ([Adrian Crook, 2015](https://adriancrook.com/?p=4922); MEDIUM on the logic, old source). iOS shows the system notification dialog once per install, which is why the soft ask exists ([Iterable](https://iterable.com/blog/when-to-request-permission-to-send-push-notifications/); MEDIUM), and the iOS rating prompt appears at most three times per 365 days ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/115193); MEDIUM).

The early-game patterns you recognize each have a stated purpose. Fast early level-ups prove progress quickly; one mechanic at a time avoids overload; gifts of soft and premium currency seed the economy; small per-step tutorial rewards and confetti on first wins create a good feeling; an early daily reward builds the return habit; early avatar or base customization builds attachment ([Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/optimizing-ftue/); [Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/how-to-build-in-retention-and-player-engagement-as-part-of-your-early-concept); MEDIUM). The honest limit is that no source measured the effect size of any one of these, and designers state the intent as "reduce early quitting" rather than "maximize long-term retention". A cautionary snippet suggests an over-restrictive onboarding can inflate day-1 retention while day 7 collapses because players are unprepared ([iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/?p=9760); LOW). Genre-specific onboarding (gacha first-pull, 4X protection shields, match-3 early ease) found no usable sources.

Retention is steep, and the numbers explain why the first session is guarded so closely. **D1/D7/D30** mean the share of installers who return on day 1, 7 and 30. GameAnalytics data for 2025 (16,262 games) puts the all-genre median near **22% at D1, just under 4% at D7 and about 0.7% at D30**; the top 10% of games reach about 40%, 11-12% and about 4% ([GameDevReports summary of GameAnalytics](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game); HIGH as reported, with two flags: the summary post may be sponsored and the primary report was not opened). Computed from those medians, the typical game loses about 80% of its remaining players between D1 and D7 and another 80% between D7 and D30. Adjust reports a higher all-genre D1 of 27% for 2025 ([Adjust via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report); MEDIUM); the two datasets measure different app populations and the sources do not reconcile them. Do not trust genre tables. The only genre breakdown that is both recent and sourced is casual and puzzle on Android from 2022 AppsFlyer data ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/); MEDIUM), while other published grids contradict each other by large factors: one puts RPG D30 near 3.5% ([Segwise, attributing Mistplay](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies.md); LOW, original not found) and another puts it at 15-25% ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/retention-by-game-genre); LOW), a range above even GameAnalytics' top-1% D30 of 13-15%.

Why players leave in week one clusters into four groups: fun arrives too late, friction before play (accounts, ads, prompts, long tutorials), difficulty or technical problems, and a mismatch between the ad and the game. The best list is a 2015 opinion piece ([Game Developer, 16 reasons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/16-reasons-why-players-are-leaving-your-game); HIGH that these are the author's claims, MEDIUM as current guidance), and no source ranks the causes with data. On the ad mismatch, a survey of 4,500 US players found gameplay quality is the top reason people download and keep playing a game (67%), and that ad accuracy matters about twice as much to Android users as to iOS users ([GameDevReports on Mistplay and AppsFlyer](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/mistplay-and-appsflyer-player-loyalty); MEDIUM, self-reported). The same survey lists engagement bonuses (64%), in-game events (50%) and frequent content updates (34%) as the top reasons players return, which matches the machinery described next.

## The patterns you notice are return reasons stacked at three time scales

Sensor Tower organizes top-grossing live operations ("live ops": the schedule of events, offers and content a game runs after launch) into layers by cadence: daily recurring events (missions, check-ins, win streaks), weekly feature events (passes, mini-games, treasure hunts), and multi-week seasonal events (collections, competitions, alliance championships) ([Sensor Tower, March 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-grossing-mobile-games-live-ops-strategies-2025-report); HIGH). Royal Match shows the whole stack: daily pressure events (Lava Quest, Jungle Treasure), a weekly rhythm of contests and team events, and two-month collections and passes, with revenue spikes on weekends that Sensor Tower attributes to event-driven conversion rather than more players ([Sensor Tower, May 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/what-royal-matchs-calendar-teaches-about-live-ops-in-2026); HIGH for the calendar, MEDIUM for the causal claim). Each layer answers a different question: why open the game today, why this week, why this season. Naavik adds that top casual puzzle games have converged on three shared tools, a daily jackpot, leaderboard events and endless album collections, and that cross-title relative retention from day 1 to day 90 has converged near 25% with about 38 minutes of play a day (iOS, US only) ([Naavik, Nov 2025](https://naavik.co/digest/live-ops-trends-powering-mobile-puzzle/); HIGH).

The only prevalence study found is a 2025 preprint that coded the 40 top mobile games in South Korea (the selection criterion was not confirmed). Daily login appeared in 38 of 40, loot boxes in 37, battle passes in 31, progress boosters in 27, social features in 26, conflict-driven design in 25, first-purchase benefits in 17, collection gacha in 12 and time-gated progression in 9 ([arXiv 2504.10714](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10714); MEDIUM). Three flags apply: one country, a sample skewed toward gacha RPG and strategy, and the paper's text counts time-gated progression as 10 games while its table says 9. The table below gives each recurring pattern its industry name and an example you may have played.

|---|---|---|---|

Several of these mechanics come with sourced details worth knowing. Pure login rewards pay for opening the app rather than playing, which is why top puzzle games replaced them with the daily jackpot ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/live-ops-trends-powering-mobile-puzzle/); HIGH). Duolingo's A/B test of separating the daily goal from the streak raised relative day-14 retention by 3.3%, and users with a 7-day streak were 2.4 times more likely to return the next day, a correlation that does not prove the streak caused it ([Duolingo, 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak); HIGH as reported); it is the only primary-source streak experiment the notes found, and no game company publishes an equivalent. Timers and lives are softening: Royal Match hands out unlimited lives as an event reward, and Supercell tested instant and 24-hour chests in January 2025 with total reward value held constant ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/what-royal-matchs-calendar-teaches-about-live-ops-in-2026); [Supercell](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashroyale/blog/news/chest-experiment-king-tower-levels-1-9); HIGH). Endless albums removed the finite-content ceiling that caused boom-and-bust engagement, at the price of a perpetual reward source: Royal Match offset it with roughly a 30% cut in coin rewards from level completions ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/live-ops-trends-powering-mobile-puzzle/); HIGH). Prestige exists for two stated reasons, a "ladder climbing" feeling of power and a way to bring ever-growing numbers back to a manageable scale ([Game Developer, 2017](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii); HIGH). Pity systems cap an otherwise unbounded random spend, which makes them both a trust feature and a regulatory shield; that motive is an inference (MEDIUM), partly supported by odds-disclosure rules. Be careful with gacha numbers: "soft pity" (odds rising near pull 75) is a community estimate, not an official figure ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/genshin-impact-pity-soft-pity-systems-explained); LOW).

Push notifications are the outbound arm of all of this. Vendors categorize game pushes as early churn prevention, habit-building (daily bonus, streak warnings), conversion, dormant re-engagement and limited-time offers ([Pushwoosh](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/game-app-push-notifications/); MEDIUM, a vendor with a commercial interest). Android 13 made notification permission opt-in, and vendors report a large drop in opt-in as a result ([Pushwoosh research](https://pushwoosh.com/blog/android-13-opt-in-rates-research); LOW). Every game-push opt-in benchmark comes from a push vendor, and one vendor sample found no reliable link between opt-in rate and day-1 retention ([Pushwoosh](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/game-apps-metrics/); LOW). The reasonable reading (MEDIUM, inference) is that push amplifies in-game appointment hooks and does not replace them.

## Each hook leans on a human tendency, but the proof comes from loyalty cards and slot machines

No peer-reviewed source maps game features to psychology one to one, so the table below is a synthesis: each lever is tied to a finding cited here or to a dark-pattern study that names the feature, and the link from feature to lever is this report's reading (MEDIUM at best). Four terms recur. **Endowed progress** means people try harder when they are told they have already started; a car-wash loyalty-card experiment found 34% redemption for a card with two stamps already added versus 19% for a blank card needing the same purchases ([Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-lowdown-on-customer-loyalty-programs-which-are-the-most-effective-and-why/); MEDIUM, secondary summary). The **goal gradient** means effort speeds up as the reward gets closer, shown with coffee-shop stamp cards ([Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration-illusionary-goal); HIGH for the study's existence). **Variable-ratio rewards** pay out after an unpredictable number of tries, the schedule behind slot machines; the application to loot boxes is an analogy in textbook sources, not a loot-box experiment ([Lumen Learning](https://content.one.lumenlearning.com/introductiontopsychology/chapter/reading-reinforcement-schedules/); MEDIUM). A **near-miss** is a result just short of a win; in a 2009 brain-imaging study it felt worse than a plain miss yet increased the urge to keep playing and activated reward circuitry, but only when players felt in control, and the press claim that this "releases dopamine" is interpretation ([Clark et al., Neuron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2929454); HIGH for the main finding, small sample).

| Feature | What the designer wants | Psychological lever | How solid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily login reward | A daily-open habit | Prompt plus ability plus motivation ([Fogg model](https://behaviormodel.org/); HIGH); goal gradient across a 7- or 30-day calendar; loss avoidance when a missed day resets the streak | Lever links MEDIUM. China's December 2023 draft rules proposed banning daily login rewards, which shows regulators treat them as a hook ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2023/12/22/draft-gaming-regulations-cause-sharp-fall-in-netease-and-tencent-share-prices/); MEDIUM, draft withdrawn) |
| Streak | Daily return | Loss avoidance, endowed progress once the streak is above zero | Loss aversion is contested (see below); effect in games untested |
| Timers, energy, lives | Short frequent sessions; sell "skip the wait" | Scarcity and impatience; labelled "Playing by Appointment" and "Pay to Skip" dark patterns in a 2022 heuristic evaluation ([deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/articles/finding-dark-patterns-in-casual-mobile-games-using-heuristic-evaluation)) | MEDIUM |
| Gacha, loot box | Spending on chance | Variable ratio, near-miss, curiosity, opaque virtual currency | Near-miss evidence is from slot machines, not gacha; no source tests whether pity raises or lowers spending |
| Battle pass | Month-long commitment | Goal gradient, endowed progress (tiers visible and pre-filled), time-limited scarcity; sunk cost is a standard bias but unsourced here | MEDIUM; loyalty-card data, not game data |
| Collection, album | "Finish the set" | Completion motivation ([Quantic Foundry](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025742/A-Deep-Dive-into-the); MEDIUM) and ownership (Octalysis) | The mapping is the notes' inference |
| Clan, guild | Obligation and belonging | Relatedness, one of three needs in Self-Determination Theory ([Ryan, Rigby, Przybylski 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf); HIGH) plus social obligation ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/); MEDIUM) | Reciprocity is a standard principle but unsourced here |
| Starter pack | Cheap first purchase | Barrier removal, anchoring, scarcity | EU consumer authorities list time-limited offers as a pressure tactic ([Forbrukertilsynet](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/eng-articles/national-consumer-authorities-and-commission-take-action-on-harmful-practices-in-the-gaming-industry); MEDIUM) |

| Limited-time event | Urgency | Scarcity, fear of missing out, social proof via leaderboards | MEDIUM |
| Progress bar, "1 of 5 done" tutorial | Finish the onboarding | Endowed progress, goal gradient | Transfer from loyalty cards to games is plausible but untested |

Two popular explanations are weaker than they sound. The Zeigarnik effect (unfinished tasks are remembered better) failed to replicate in a 2025 study, though the tendency to resume an interrupted task held up, so the safe claim is "unfinished tasks pull you back", not "unfinished tasks stick in memory" ([Ghibellini and Meier 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html); MEDIUM). Loss aversion, the idea that losses weigh about twice as much as gains, is disputed: a large study found people of all experience levels loss averse ([Mrkva et al. 2020](https://imo.unisg.ch/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/01_jcpy.1156.pdf); MEDIUM), while a 2022 study that removed confounds found none for small amounts ([Judgment and Decision Making 2022](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/22/220414/jdm220414.html); MEDIUM). Streak-loss hooks probably still work because a streak feels large relative to one day's effort, but that is this report's inference (LOW). The frameworks designers cite differ in standing. Fogg's behavior model and Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness each predicted enjoyment and future play) have academic backing ([Rochester copy of the 2006 paper](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf); HIGH). The Hook model (trigger, action, variable reward, investment) and Octalysis (eight "core drives") are practitioner playbooks without controlled game trials ([dsebastien summary](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/hook-model/); [UZH Quartz](https://www.gbl.uzh.ch/quartz/definitions/Octalysis-Framework); MEDIUM). Bartle's four player types came from a 1989-1990 discussion among UK MUD players and Bartle himself calls them "stereotypical" ([Bartle](https://mud.co.uk/richard/hcds.htm); HIGH).

## Social features are widely believed to help retention, and nobody can quote a trustworthy number

Clans, friends, gifting and co-op are the standard social set. Naavik argues clans work through progression (clan level), return triggers (daily donation limits, clan events) and social obligation (event progress, item donations), and that clan features often lift cosmetic and VIP sales because social players care about status ([Naavik, 2022](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/); HIGH that this is claimed, MEDIUM as evidence because it is expert opinion without data). The strongest hard evidence is a 2019 thesis analyzing two commercial free-to-play games: social-feature use in the first seven days was associated with higher retention at day 30 in one game and showed no such link in the other ([University of Jyvaskyla](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/66822); HIGH that it says this, MEDIUM on generalization, correlational, n=2). A practitioner with 50+ launches writes that players with at least one in-game connection churn more slowly in every title he has worked on, "though I have never seen a defensible published coefficient for it" ([Game Growth Advisor, 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/); HIGH that this is what the source says, MEDIUM as evidence). Blog multipliers for friend or guild retention circulate widely, but the notes could not trace any of them, and a cited research paper argues raw gaps are inflated by self-selection (engaged players join guilds); this report therefore leaves those multipliers out and treats them as marketing (LOW).

Competitive structure has better research, though not from mobile games. Engagement-optimized matchmaking (choosing opponents by predicted churn instead of skill alone) improved retained players by only 0.3% to 1.1% in an Electronic Arts simulation ([arXiv 1702.06820](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.06820); MEDIUM). A study of 5.4 million Lichess chess games found matchmaking that accounts for recent wins and losses raised engagement 4-6% over pure skill matching, and that a loss streak can push players to quit for the night ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/gaming-study-says-skill-based-matchmaking-is-fair-but-it-also-quietly-drives-players-away/); HIGH as reported, MEDIUM on transfer to mobile). So hidden win-rate tuning is a real research direction with small published gains, but the notes found no source confirming that any specific mobile game ships it, and this report does not assert that any does. Alliances in 4X and survival games gate build help, buffs and event access, so leaving has a cost ([BlueStacks guide](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/white-out-survival/wos-alliance-guide-en.html); MEDIUM for the mechanic). The shared design logic is a team goal plus a daily contribution limit, which turns a solo habit into a promise to other people (inference, MEDIUM), and the dark side is that a lapsing player now hurts the team (inference, unquantified).

Social features cost money and need density. Clans raise server costs and complexity and need enough concurrent players to function ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/); MEDIUM). A moderation-industry survey of 2,408 gamers found 19% spend less and 21% play less to avoid harassment ([Game Industry Library](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/toxicity-and-the-bottom-line-2024); MEDIUM, industry-published, report not opened). Invite loops are modest: the viral coefficient K (invites per user times the share of invites that convert) is realistically 0.3-0.5 for most products, enough to lower acquisition cost but rarely enough to grow on its own, and K above 1 is generally limited to products where inviting is the point ([Echai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/retention-referral/what-s-a-viral-coefficient-and-is-chasing-a-k-factor-above-1-realistic-for-my-kind-of-product); MEDIUM, marketing blogs without primary data). A small game should therefore start with asynchronous social features before clans or chat (inference from the Naavik and Game Growth Advisor sources, MEDIUM).

## Money explains why the patterns repeat: a few payers must repay every install

Mobile game money comes from in-app purchases (IAP), ads, or both. Global mobile game IAP was about **$81.75B in 2025, up 1.3%**, gross of store fees and excluding Android China and direct web shops, with hybridcasual the one fast-growing segment (reported by a secondary summary at +20% to $4.2B) ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-state-of-the-mobile); HIGH for the headline, MEDIUM for the segment split). AppMagic's genre totals show strategy, puzzle and simulation growing while RPG and casino shrank ([AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization); HIGH); its overall totals use a different scope and period than Sensor Tower's and should not be compared with them. Hyper-casual is almost all ads, casual leans toward IAP, and hybrid-casual is described as roughly half and half ([Sensor Tower State of Mobile Gaming 2025](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/sensor_tower__state_of_mobile_gaming_2025__en.pdf); MEDIUM, read through a summary). The practical consequence for the player is that you see the engine of the genre: ad-led games interrupt, IAP-led games sell time-skips, packs and pulls, and hybrids do both (inference, MEDIUM).

Spending is extremely concentrated. Moloco's data on 4,000+ games shows the **top 2% of players generate 35-45% of in-app purchase revenue and the top 10% generate 70-85%** ([Moloco via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/moloco-mobile-market-status-and-growth); HIGH). That concentration is why offers are tuned to find and keep the likely big spenders while ads and free rewards serve everyone else. There is no free, current, authoritative benchmark for what share of players ever pay or for revenue per daily active user (ARPDAU); the figures in circulation are unattributed vendor ranges, so this report gives none. A 2019 benchmark only supports that even top projects in most genres rarely convert more than about 1% ([GameAnalytics 2019 via WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-2261); LOW, old). Offer design follows one recurring logic: a cheap, time-boxed, high-value first purchase, then ladders upward with bundles, passes and personalized offers. Evidence that this raises conversion is vendor-published, and the notes found no citable data for price anchoring, "best value" tags or daily deals ([Heroic Labs](https://heroiclabs.com/blog/understanding-piggy-bank-mechanics-mobile-games/); MEDIUM for the mechanic only). One fact is sourced: the average first purchase in top US RPGs is $8.30 on Google Play versus $17.90 on the App Store, so payers are segmented by platform ([AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization); HIGH).

The in-game economy ties it together. A **soft currency** (coins) is earned by playing and spent on routine progress, while a **hard currency** (gems) is mostly bought with money and used to skip waits or buy exclusives; designers balance faucets (rewards in) against sinks (spending out) because faucets exceeding sinks causes inflation ([Adrian Crook, 2016](https://adriancrook.com/?p=4918); MEDIUM; [MWM glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/game-economy); LOW). Time and scarcity are what hard currency sells: timers, energy and caps create friction that payment removes (inference, MEDIUM). Because soft-currency generosity sets how often a player feels pressure to pay, economy tuning is also a retention lever, but no quantified evidence exists. Ads follow the same pain-point logic: rewarded video (opt-in) is placed when a player runs out of lives, fails a level or lacks currency, and interstitials (forced) are capped conservatively ([Unity/ironSource](https://unity.com/blog/3-ways-to-optimize-the-ad-experience-for-your-users); MEDIUM). One vendor test that added interstitials to a rewarded-only game raised revenue 17% and ARPDAU 18% while cutting D7 retention 2.3% ([ironSource case](https://is.com/?p=52105); MEDIUM, vendor case). The claim that players who watch rewarded ads retain and pay more is correlational, and the vendor concedes that players who choose to watch are probably more engaged ([Unity/ironSource, 2022](https://unity.com/blog/understanding-the-impact-of-rewarded-ads-on-iap-retention-and-engagement); MEDIUM-LOW, so LOW as causal evidence).

Platform fees are changing where purchases happen. An April 2025 court ruling and a December 2025 appeals decision let US developers link players to web stores, with Apple allowed to charge only a "reasonable" commission that had not been set as of 2026-08-11, and the Supreme Court agreed in June 2026 to review the contempt finding ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/apples-fight-over-commissions-for-linked-out-app-store-purchases-continues-in-district-court/); HIGH). Bain reports 74% of top-grossing mobile games ran their own web store in 2026, versus 12% in 2019 ([Bain Gaming Report 2026](https://www.bain.com/insights/game-distribution-half-your-players-already-buy-direct-gaming-report-2026/); HIGH for the adoption figure, MEDIUM for its margin estimate of 15-30 points per direct transaction). AppMagic measures a different slice (US top-100, "tools" rather than own stores) and shows a dip, so the two do not contradict each other. Google's fee structure is reported inconsistently (20% baseline per Bain versus a tiered structure in news reports) and is unreconciled (LOW).

## Marketing buys the installs, and retention decides whether the purchase pays back

The funnel runs: ad impression (priced per 1,000 views, **CPM**) to click (**CTR**, the share of viewers who click) to install (priced as **CPI**, cost per install) to day-1, day-7 and day-30 retention to revenue (**LTV**, lifetime revenue per installed player). **ROAS** is revenue divided by ad spend, and **payback** is the number of days until a cohort's revenue equals what it cost to acquire. A game scales only if LTV beats CPI by enough margin. Adjust's data for the top 5,000 apps (January 2024 to January 2026) shows a global average CPI of $0.56 (up 30%), with North America at $1.68, Europe at $0.53 and Latin America at $0.14 ([Adjust via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report); MEDIUM, read through a secondary summary). The median ratio of paid to organic installs is about 3.3 to 1, so most installs are bought rather than found. Global gaming user-acquisition spend was about $25B in 2025, with nearly half in the US and China-headquartered publishers holding 35% of spend outside China ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/gaming-marketing/); HIGH). Because the typical player is gone by day 7, the average player must produce more than the CPI through a small paying fraction, which is the economic reason every game uses the retention and monetization stack described above (inference, MEDIUM). Payback windows differ by model: short for ad-led games (days to about a month), long for strategy and IAP-heavy games (90 to 180 days), so slow-payback genres build deep long-run systems like alliances, events and passes (inference, MEDIUM; the window numbers themselves are glossary-grade, [MWM](https://mwm.ai/glossary/payback-period), LOW). Liftoff's data shows how much the platform matters: average day-30 ROAS for casual games is 47% on iOS versus 15% on Android ([Liftoff 2025](https://liftoff.ai/?p=34267); MEDIUM, a vendor with a large dataset).

Paid channels are dominated by self-optimizing ad platforms (Meta, Google, TikTok, AppLovin, Unity, Mintegral, Moloco) where the creative does most of the targeting, so teams produce enormous numbers of ad variants and keep the winners. Top gaming advertisers (over $4M a quarter) produced 2,400-2,600 creative variations per quarter, up 25-30% ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/gaming-marketing/); HIGH), and one secondhand report says the top 2% of creatives took 53% of ad spend ([AppSamurai](https://appsamurai.com/blog/how-to-market-test-a-mobile-game/); MEDIUM). That explains why the ads look alike: a few concepts win repeatedly and every studio converges on them. Unity's hyper-casual guide advises showing a "fail" in the first 3-4 seconds and says 70-80% of Supersonic's most successful creatives did ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/growing-hyper-casual-games); MEDIUM, publisher claim, undated). The mismatch between an ad and the real game is a deliberate trade: marketing optimizes installs per dollar while the product team owns retention (inference, MEDIUM). Google Play bans ads that do not accurately reflect the app, and the UK advertising regulator acted against Playrix ads ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/fake-ads-and-mobile-gaming-how-studios-balance-clickbait-gameplay-integrity-and-profitability-cm7j2hc9s00bvip0l0r4k59lw); LOW). The retention and cost numbers circulating about "fake gameplay" ads trace to one vendor blog and are not used here. AI has multiplied creative volume further; AppsFlyer ties 25-30% growth in creative output to AI and writes that the result is "not a shortage of creativity, but a surplus of it" ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/gaming-marketing/); HIGH), so differentiation shifts to original concepts and the real product (inference, MEDIUM).

Before a studio builds, it tests the ad. The sequence is a concept test (a video of the idea run as ads, measuring CPI and installs per 1,000 impressions, **IPM**), then a playable build in a **soft launch** (a trial release in one or two cheaper markets), where D1, D7, D30 and LTV versus CPI act as go or no-go gates, and finally a scale test that checks CPI does not jump when spend rises. Practitioners agree on that logic but not on the numbers: gate thresholds from different advisors conflict, and the hyper-casual CPI targets of 2020-21 are not transferable to 2026 midcore markets ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/); LOW; [Unity](https://unity.com/resources/growing-hyper-casual-games); MEDIUM, old). The free or cheap levers have some evidence. Store-page tests lifted one game's conversion from 20.46% to 24.42% by changing the icon ([SplitMetrics case](https://splitmetrics.com/cases/ciliz-improves-conversion-with-splitmetrics-agency/); LOW, vendor marketing). Google Play pre-registration with a reward raised the share of pre-registrants who installed by 20% and gave almost 50% higher day-60 retention in one Nexon game ([Google Play Console case study](https://play.google.com/console/about/nexon-casestudy/); MEDIUM, Google-hosted success story from 2019). Apple and Google run featuring nominations with weeks of lead time ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/contact/app-store/promote/); HIGH for the process). Buzz does not substitute for retention: Supercell's Squad Busters drew about 3.2B TikTok views in six weeks and, per analyst estimates, cost about $150M in total before shutting down on 30 October 2025 ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/06/19/squad-busters-revenue-28-million-paid-ads-supercell); [WN Hub summary of Deconstructor of Fun](https://wnhub.io/ru/news/games/item-49267); MEDIUM, analyst estimates, original not retrieved).

Privacy rules changed how this is measured. Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) pop-up asks iOS users for permission to track; about 38-39% of gaming users said yes in Q1 2025 and Q1 2026 ([Adjust via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report); MEDIUM). Everyone else is measured through aggregated, delayed Apple postbacks (SKAdNetwork, now AdAttributionKit), so iOS cohort revenue is partly modeled, which is why early-day predictions and "creative as targeting" matter (inference, MEDIUM). Google announced in October 2025 that it would retire most Privacy Sandbox technologies, leaving Android on its advertising ID for now ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/chrome-kills-most-privacy-sandbox-technologies-after-adoption-fails/); MEDIUM). After acquisition, customer relationship management (CRM) takes over: push, email and retargeting ads target lapsed players, with escalating comeback offers by dormancy segment, and "push, deep link, offer" now doubles as a margin tactic when the link goes to a web shop. The evidence is almost entirely from tool vendors, there are no controlled win-back uplift numbers, and casual and hyper-casual titles have little to gain from web shops while live-ops RPG, 4X and casino titles gain the most ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-05-05-mobile-game-d2c-web-shop-strategy-2026/); LOW).

## Regulators are targeting hidden prices, hidden odds and children, not engagement itself

The harm evidence is real but correlational. A meta-analysis found loot box spending correlates with problem-gambling symptoms at r of about 0.26, a small to moderate link, and the direction of cause is unresolved: gambling-prone people may spend more on loot boxes, loot boxes may cause harm, or a third factor may drive both ([Garea et al. via figshare](https://figshare.utas.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Meta-analysis_of_the_relationship_between_problem_gambling_excessive_gaming_and_loot_box_spending/22996898/1/files/40746986.pdf); MEDIUM, abstract-level). A study of 726 collectible-card-game players found booster-pack spending had no significant link to problem gambling in physical stores and a trivial one online ([Zendle et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8023484); HIGH, with sample caveats), and a 2025 longitudinal study reported baseline loot box spending predicted starting conventional gambling six months later ([BMC Psychology 2025](https://biomedcentral-bmcpsychology.publicaciones.saludcastillayleon.es/articles/10.1186/s40359-025-02766-1); MEDIUM). UK government reviewed the evidence in 2022 and found a "stable and consistent" association but could not establish cause ([Bird and Bird](https://mediawrites.twobirds.com/post/102j2z6/loot-boxes-uk-government-says-no-to-regulation); MEDIUM). "Dark patterns" in games, a term from Zagal, Bjork and Lewis (2013), are design elements where the creator's interest and the player's diverge, and an analysis of user data on 1,496 games found them widespread, including in games thought benign ([arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05039); MEDIUM).

Penalties so far attach to deception and minors. The US Federal Trade Commission and Department of Justice settled with Cognosphere (Genshin Impact) in January 2025 for a $20 million penalty and terms barring loot box sales to under-16s without parental consent and requiring truthful odds and cost disclosure and a direct US-dollar purchase option; the court entry date is unconfirmed ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse); HIGH for the terms). Epic Games (Fortnite) paid $245 million in refunds for "dark patterns" such as confusing buttons that led to unwanted charges, plus $275 million for children's privacy violations ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/80648); HIGH). South Korea fined Nexon Korea 11.6 billion won for lowering gacha drop rates without telling players, including cases of 0% ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/nexon-fined-over-8-million-for-manipulating-item-drop-rates-in-games/); MEDIUM). Apple has required odds disclosure since December 2017 and Google Play since May 2019, though the current text was not rechecked ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/apple-now-requires-disclosure-of-loot-box-odds); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/google-play-developers-must-now-disclose-loot-box-odds); MEDIUM).

The picture as of 2026-10-22 is a patchwork moving toward price transparency and child protection. The EU's consumer-protection network announced eleven coordinated actions against ten named game companies on 30 September 2026, asking them to bring in-game virtual currency practices into line with EU consumer law, with particular attention to variable-reward systems, dark patterns and direct exhortation to children; the actions are not findings of breach ([joint statement](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/75ff33d4-77a6-4f14-916a-766a8c6c281c_en?filename=CPC+Network+Joint+Statement+of+the+Consumer+Protection+Cooperation+Network+on+online+games.pdf); HIGH, read as a PDF; company names not used here). The Commission is reported to intend a Digital Fairness Act proposal in November 2026, expected to cover dark patterns, addictive design, loot boxes and virtual currencies, but the text is unpublished and earlier timing statements slipped, so its content is expectation only (LOW) ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/15/eu-consumer-protection-network-launches-action-involving-nine-video-games-compani-102o5do); [Heuking](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/digital-fairness-act-implementation-obligations-companies-need-to-prepare-for.html)). Brazil's Law 15.211/2025 prohibits loot boxes in games accessible to minors and has been in force since 17 March 2026, with enforcement practice not yet found ([Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/en/recent-publications/publications/digital-law/law-15-211-25-protection-for-children-and-adolescents-in-the-digital-environment); MEDIUM). China's December 2023 draft that proposed banning daily login rewards was withdrawn in January 2024 and no final rules were found ([RTTNews](https://www.rttnews.com/3418482/china-withdraws-online-gaming-restrictions-draft.aspx); MEDIUM). The UK still relies on industry self-regulation and the notes found no update after 2023 (MEDIUM, [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/loot-boxes-11-new-principles-for-an-industry-led-approach)).

No consensus line separates engaging from exploitative design. Three tests recur and are the notes' synthesis of Zagal, Self-Determination Theory and the FTC and EU reasoning, not a published standard (MEDIUM): would the player, shown exactly how the mechanic works and what it costs, still choose it; does it work through anxiety, shortage or obligation rather than competence, relatedness and enjoyment; and is the audience likely to include children. Mechanics regulators have actually challenged sit at the exploitative end (paid random rewards sold to minors, currency bundles that obscure price, time-limited pressure aimed at children), while progress bars, social play and mastery loops are largely uncontested in the sources found. The designer-side partner to this is the harmonious-versus-obsessive distinction from Self-Determination Theory research: high need satisfaction was hypothesized to produce "harmonious" passion and low need satisfaction "obsessive" passion ([Przybylski et al. 2009 record](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/having-to-versus-wanting-to-play-background-and-consequences-of-h/); MEDIUM, hypothesis only, results not read).

## Vietnam and Southeast Asia: huge downloads, thin payer share, strategy and local RPGs on top

Vietnam's mobile game revenue was about $825M in 2025 (up 9.16%) with roughly 54M gamers, of whom only 7-10% spend and 2-3% are high spenders, per a Gamota report ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/vietnams-mobile-games-market-reached-825m-in-revenue-in-2025); MEDIUM, read via search summary because the page returned an error). Sensor Tower's Q1 2025 data puts Southeast Asia at 1.93B installs and $625M of in-app purchases (excluding direct web payments, which are common in the region), with Vietnam at 329M installs, Roblox leading downloads, and 4X strategy (+51.5%), MMORPG and squad RPG leading revenue; Last War: Survival and the local RPG Big Bang Thoi Khong lead Vietnam's revenue ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-southeast-asia-mobile?open=false); MEDIUM). A $1.66B figure for all Vietnamese gaming revenue is a different scope from the $825M mobile figure ([Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1716994/viet-nam-targets-us-2-4-billion-in-gaming-industry-revenue-by-2029.html); MEDIUM), and the sources do not say so explicitly. Mobile Legends: Bang Bang has stayed top-5 in the region since 2017 through localization, regional characters, tournaments, influencers and local offices ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-southeast-asia-mobile?open=false); MEDIUM). On the supply side, Vietnamese studios reached 4.9B downloads, which measures games made in Vietnam rather than played there ([80.lv summary](https://80.lv/articles/vietnam-s-game-winning-era); LOW).

For marketing, practitioner estimates put Android casual CPI at $0.20-0.60 in Southeast Asia and $0.08-0.30 in India, with India described as volume with the weakest value transfer ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/); LOW, one author's estimates). TikTok claims 59% of users discovered new games through creative content on its platform ([Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1716994/viet-nam-targets-us-2-4-billion-in-gaming-industry-revenue-by-2029.html); LOW, vendor claim). Vietnam's Decree 147/2024, in force around 25 December 2024, requires verified player accounts and parent or guardian management of under-16 accounts, and reports conflict on the under-18 time limits (180 minutes per day across games, with one hour per session or 60 minutes per day per game depending on the source) ([AP via Bangkok Post](https://bangkokpost.com/world/2926912/sweeping-vietnam-internet-law-comes-into-force); [Gamota](https://gamota.com/en_GB/?p=3364%2F); MEDIUM, conflicting). The notes found no Vietnamese rules on loot boxes or gacha, no Vietnam-specific CPI, retention or ARPU benchmarks, and no data on Zalo or Facebook as game ad channels. Two inferences follow, both unsourced and so LOW to MEDIUM: a Vietnamese indie team can run cheap concept tests in a low-CPI Southeast Asian or Latin American market and soft-launch in Southeast Asia plus one English-speaking market rather than the US, and the low payer share plus local payment channels probably make web or local-payment shops more important in Vietnam than the global average.

## Where the evidence runs out

The strongest material describes structure: what top-grossing games ship (Sensor Tower, Naavik, the Korean top-40 coding), how revenue is concentrated (Moloco), what regulators have done (FTC, EU network), and the retention distribution of all mobile games (GameAnalytics). The weakest parts, in rough order of how much they matter to the user's question, are these. First, the effect size of any single hook: no source gives a causal retention or revenue lift for daily login, streaks, energy, battle passes or onboarding patterns, and the only primary-source streak experiment is from a language-learning app. Second, the guild and friend retention advantage: qualitative support plus a two-game thesis, and the blog multipliers are untraceable. Third, genre-level retention and payer-conversion benchmarks: published grids conflict by large factors, the strongest figures are all-genre distributions, and no free current table of payer share or ARPDAU exists. Fourth, the psychology: loss aversion is contested, the Zeigarnik memory effect failed to replicate, and the feature-to-lever mapping is a synthesis built on loyalty-card and slot-machine studies. Fifth, marketing numbers: most "benchmarks" come from vendors selling UA, ASO or analytics tools, the original Mobile Dev Memo and Deconstructor of Fun posts were not retrieved, and soft-launch gates conflict between advisors. Sixth, regulation moves fast: the Digital Fairness Act text is unpublished, the Apple commission is unset, and Vietnam, Japan and the UK have gaps. The notes also carry specific unresolved contradictions that this report did not smooth over: the Korean paper's text versus its table; Genshin pity as 50% after 89 pulls (2020 article) versus hard pity at 90 (community); Bain's 74% web-store adoption versus AppMagic's decline measured on a different base; Bain's 20% Google baseline fee versus tiered news reports.

## Conclusion

The central insight for someone who plays many games is that the repeated patterns are not creative accidents; they are the visible half of one system. The invisible half is the spreadsheet in which every install has a price, a few payers repay it over a payback window, and every hook (login gift, timer, album, pass, alliance) is a reason to stay long enough for that repayment, which is why games from different studios and countries converge on the same daily, weekly and seasonal machinery. That reframes "how do I hook players" into a design question with an ordering: fun in minutes, a goal, a reason to return at each time scale, then a monetization offer, and only then marketing to feed it. It also shows why the market moved from login gifts to play-to-claim jackpots and from finite seasonal collections to endless albums: each change fixed a measurable weakness of the earlier pattern (rewarding the open instead of the play; a content ceiling that caused boom-and-bust cycles), even though the size of the gain was never published.

The second takeaway is a caution about confidence. Almost everything quantitative that sounds authoritative in blogs (multipliers for guilds, retention for pass holders, conversion lifts for offers) lacks a source, while the best-evidenced facts are mundane: retention is steep, spending is concentrated, ads converge on a few concepts, and regulators punish concealment and targeting of children rather than engagement. A builder can adopt the structure with high confidence, set their own numbers from their own cohorts, and treat any benchmark that does not name its dataset as a hypothesis. If the report is used to design features, keep odds disclosure, real-money price display and parental gating as a floor, because every jurisdiction surveyed is moving that way.
