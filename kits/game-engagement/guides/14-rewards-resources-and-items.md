# Rewards, Resources and Items

Everything a player earns, holds or spends is one of four things: a balance, a stack of identical items, an individual item with attributes of its own, or a counter that is never spent. This guide sorts each kind, sets rules for how many currencies a game carries and how they convert, expire and show their real-money value, sizes small rewards and their presentation, covers items and duplicates, and shows how to keep faucets and sinks in balance and audit them from the ledger of `billing.virtual-currency`. Earning models and offers are in `03-economy-and-offers.md`, reward cadence in `07-hook-and-rhythm.md`, objective ladders and XP curves in `08-objectives-and-progression.md`, screens in `10-screens-and-navigation.md`, missions in `13-missions-and-activities.md`, dashboards in `12-live-ops-and-instrumentation.md`, and manipulation techniques, odds and the legal line in `15-manipulation-and-the-line.md`.

Evidence: Apple, Google Play, the EU consumer-protection network (CPC) and the FTC, read on 2026-10-27; peer-reviewed studies; designer writing from Game Developer, Naavik, GameRefinery and studio blogs; each named inline with HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW. No source gives a recommended number of currencies, or reward amounts per step, level or day, so every size below is a kit recommendation and says so.

## Decision rule

1. **Sort every resource by what the ledger stores.** A currency is a balance; a stackable item (booster, key, shard) is a count under an item code; both pass `grant` and `spend` with a reason of the form `<block-slug>.<action>`. An equipment piece with its own level or stats is a record the game stores itself; upgrading it is a `spend` of materials. A counter such as a trophy total is computed from play and never passes the ledger (kit recommendation, MEDIUM). The taxonomy below gives each kind its purpose, faucet, sink, price status, limit and risk.
2. **Carry as few currencies as the game needs, and count the purchasable ones first.** The CPC network says that several purchasable currencies in one game, or a purchase that needs several exchanges, make a price "particularly difficult" to understand, and lists both as practices to avoid ([CPC Key Principles, 21 March 2025, principle 2](https://esportslegal.news/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies.pdf), HIGH for the text: a copy of the Commission document, non-binding, scoped to currencies bought with real money). Designers split on earned currencies: one says extra currencies give finer control over what a behaviour pays, another that they add an illusion of choice and some confusion for first-time players, mostly in complex games ([PocketGamer.biz, undated](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/is-the-rise-of-engagement-focused-in-game-currencies-confusing-player), LOW, seen as a search summary). Shokrizade says each extra layer between the player and real money weakens price judgement and cites "research" without naming it ([Game Developer, 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-top-f2p-monetization-tricks), LOW). No source gives a maximum. Kit recommendation (LOW): one soft currency, at most one premium currency, one event currency per live event, a prestige currency only in an idle game, and everything else an item or a counter.
3. **Make every conversion one step, in one direction, at a rate the player can read.** Purchased currency may not expire ([Apple App Review Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), HIGH), and purchased virtual currency may only be used in the game it was bought for ([Google Play Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738), HIGH). A currency that can only be earned has no platform expiry rule and falls outside the CPC scope, which covers currencies bought with real money, so its expiry is a design choice. Games differ: one expires event currency at the event end and lets players trade the first event currency for a second one that buys the grand prizes ([Elvenar support](https://support.innogames.com/kb/Elvenar/en_DK/2170), MEDIUM, one game); another converts unspent event currency at four to one, capped at five hundred, into the main game and pays milestone and leaderboard rewards after the event ends for everyone ([Kano Play FAQ](https://support.kanoplay.com/hc/en/9-fubar/faq/489-what-are-limited-time-events/), MEDIUM, one game). Kit recommendation (MEDIUM): at the server `ends_at`, leftover event currency converts into a permanent resource at a rate shown from the event's first day, the cap is stated, and a leftover amount always buys something. The ledger of `billing.virtual-currency` has no expiry rule (INV-14) and spends one currency per call, so the event feature performs the conversion as a `spend` of the event currency and a `grant` of the resource, both keyed from the event's facts so a retry completes the pair.
4. **Let the player learn the real-money value of what they buy and spend.** The CPC network says the real-money price of content bought with virtual currency must be shown clearly and prominently, computed at "what the consumer would have to pay in full", without quantity discounts or promotions, however the player came by the currency (principle 1, HIGH). The FTC settlement with the Genshin Impact developer, January 2025, bars selling loot boxes for virtual currency unless players can also buy them directly with US dollars (HIGH for the terms; [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse); court entry unconfirmed, see guide 06). In an incentivized experiment with 753 UK participants, willingness to pay for loot boxes was higher in virtual currency at a one-to-one rate than in pounds ([Toccafondi, Di Paolo and Di Guida, Experimental Economics 2026](https://iris.imtlucca.it/handle/20.500.11771/42198), MEDIUM, abstract seen as a search summary). Neither the Apple 3.1.1 passages nor the Google Payments passages read here state a real-money display duty; both require odds before purchase (HIGH). Kit recommendation: every pack shows the store's price string, and every item priced in premium currency shows a real-money value at the undiscounted rate; the worksheet records where that value comes from (see Gaps). The legal line is in `15-manipulation-and-the-line.md`.
5. **Design pack sizes and remainders on purpose.** The CPC network lists as practices to avoid offering currency only in bundles that mismatch the value of purchasable content and denying the player a choice of amount, and says exploiting biases so that players overspend or hold "unneeded amounts" is likely unfair (principle 3, HIGH, non-binding). Neutral options: align the smallest pack with the cheapest common item, price items in multiples of the pack step, offer an exact top-up for a shortfall, and keep a sink at every balance so a remainder buys something (kit recommendation, MEDIUM). Measure the balance left after each purchase from the ledger.
6. **Size small rewards as a ladder priced in minutes of play.** Tiny and frequent at the step (feedback, a few soft units or none), larger per level, per session and per day, large and rare per week and season. Fixed rewards per cycle with variable surprises on top are in `07-hook-and-rhythm.md`. One practitioner method is to "reverse-engineer reward value from time" and keep the ratio steady across the progression ([Playio, 2026](https://blog.playio.co/setting-reward-values-mobile-games), LOW). Naavik says the hard part of a daily jackpot is pacing its prize pool so the economy holds ([Naavik, Nov 2025](https://naavik.co/digest/live-ops-trends-powering-mobile-puzzle/), MEDIUM, design opinion). In a meta-analysis of 128 experiments, unexpected tangible rewards and rewards unrelated to the task left intrinsic motivation unchanged, while expected rewards undermined it; effect sizes are in guide 08 ([Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/deci%201999.pdf), HIGH for the paper, LOW on transfer: lab tasks, not games). Kit reading (LOW): surprise bonuses and "stage cleared" feedback carry less risk than a payout on every tap of an activity the player already enjoys. No source gives amounts per rung (see Gaps).
7. **Present rewards with moderate juice and test the ceiling.** A 2024 review of the juicy-design studies reports that in a four-version action RPG test, medium and high juiciness raised intrinsic motivation, play time and experience, while none and too much lowered experience, play time and performance (Kao 2020); another study found juiciness raised meaning and curiosity but changed neither performance nor behaviour; an early study found no effect on the measured experience constructs; a juicy-audio study (n=61) found a medium effect on immersion and sensory fidelity that may come from more sound, not more juice; a mobile haptics study (n=38) found any vibration improved player experience and low versus high differed only in expressivity ([Hicks et al., CHI PLAY 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3677084), HIGH for what the review reports, MEDIUM as evidence; [haptics study](https://uwaterloo.ca/haptic-experience-lab/projects/juicy-haptic-design-vibrotactile-embellishments-can-improve), MEDIUM). The outcomes listed are experience, motivation, play time and performance; none is retention or spend. Kit recommendation (MEDIUM): a count-up, a sound and a short haptic on every grant, more for rare rewards, every sequence skippable with the balance credited at once.
8. **Keep faucets and sinks balanced, and give every new faucet a sink.** One studio calls an economy healthy when money keeps moving and says the stable ideal is spending premium currency at the rate it is received ([Kolibri Games, 2020](https://kolibrigames.com/blog/economy-in-free-to-play-mobile-games-part-2/), MEDIUM, one studio). A vendor calls reward inflation the most common economy problem, because live ops, promotions and rewarded acquisition layer supply on the launch calibration (Playio, LOW). Royal Match offset its endless album with a cut of about thirty percent in level-completion coins, a figure the article takes from a Reddit thread (Naavik, MEDIUM). In idle games costs grow exponentially against linear or polynomial production, and a prestige reset lets the player get further before falling behind ([Pecorella, Game Developer, 2016](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-i), HIGH). Kit recommendation: a faucet ships with a named sink or an offsetting cut in the same release. No published ladder ratio exists for other archetypes.
9. **Size the first-session gift from the first price the player sees.** In a randomized field experiment with about 575,000 new players of one football-management game, starting endowments of forty, eighty and one hundred twenty tokens were compared over a twenty-eight-day window: relative to the forty-token group, day-1 retention rose 4.5% and 5.3%, day-7 retention 6.9% and 7.7%, and first-purchase size 34.8% in the largest group (p = 0.005); daily conversion differences were not significant, no optimum was found, and two authors worked for the game's developer ([Zivic et al., PLoS ONE 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5646808/), HIGH for the design, MEDIUM on transfer: one game). GameRefinery lists the usual free first uses: a little premium currency at the start, a free speed-up with the later premium cost announced, free gacha pulls or new-player discounts ([GameRefinery, June 2022](https://gamerefinery.com/soft-landing-to-conversion-introducing-onboarding-best-practices-part-3), MEDIUM). Kit recommendation (guide 01 rule 4): the gift covers the first upgrade the tutorial points at, spent inside the tutorial.
10. **Give every item a class, a duplicate rule and a way out.** Consumables are counts that burn, permanent items stay until merged or sold, cosmetics change no outcome. Odds for paid random items come before purchase on both stores (rule 4). On duplicates, Telfer lists conversion to dust (Overwatch, Hearthstone), where players still feel the loss because dust is a fraction of the direct price; fusion, where one duplicate becomes decisive and frustrates; shards, which give clear progress, though a pack of "mere pieces" is regretted; and unlock-then-upgrade (Clash Royale), where duplicates are a sink; he adds that removing duplicates reduces depth ([Mobile Free To Play, 2018](https://mobilefreetoplay.com/creating-strong-gacha-pros-make-sure-duplicates-arent-bad-drops/), MEDIUM, one practitioner). Royal Match packs that guarantee a new card gain value as duplicates grow common near an event's end ([Sensor Tower, May 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/what-royal-matchs-calendar-teaches-about-live-ops-in-2026), HIGH that it says so). Kit recommendation: one duplicate rule per collectible type, shown before the pull, converting at a rate that buys a meaningful step.
11. **Pick what the game sells by whom the player competes against.** A vendor survey reports players three times more likely to consider spending on cosmetics than on power, and only six percent saying they would never buy cosmetics ([Qutee via Naavik, 2022](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/selling-cosmetics-part1/), LOW to MEDIUM). A survey of 519 players linked spending to unobstructed play, social interaction and economic rationale ([Hamari et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.11.045), MEDIUM, abstract seen as a search summary), so removing obstacles appears to sell (kit reading, MEDIUM). Strategy generated nearly $15B in 2025 ([Sensor Tower, March 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-grossing-mobile-games-live-ops-strategies-2025-report), HIGH), and one vendor describes its economy as time compression ([Juego Studios, 2026](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/arpdau-benchmarks-by-game-genre), LOW). Kolibri argues its idle game is not pay-to-win because progression is mostly endless (MEDIUM). No source compares revenue of cosmetic-only and power-selling designs in one genre. Kit recommendation (LOW): sell cosmetics and time where players compete head to head, and treat selling power in solo play as a choice whose sentiment cost is measured.
12. **Give every cap an exit and every timer the server clock.** No source measures how inventory caps or timed boosters affect retention or revenue. The ledger rejects a grant that would pass `LEDGER_MAX_BALANCE` with `balance_limit_exceeded`, so the caller decides what happens to the excess. Kit recommendation (LOW): a full bag or a capped balance offers sell, convert or overflow to the inbox and never destroys a reward silently; timed boosters carry the server's `ends_at` and the client counts down from `server_now`.
13. **Audit from the ledger every week.** A faucet is the sum of `grant` rows by reason and a sink the sum of `spend` rows by reason, so the reason field is the audit key; the ledger registers each reason as a faucet or sink in `currency_flows` and emits `currency_granted` and `currency_spent` with `reason` and `balance_after`. GameAnalytics models the same pair as source and sink events per currency to "detect imbalances" ([GameAnalytics docs](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/events-metrics-and-filtering/event-types/resource-events/), HIGH), and GA4 lists `earn_virtual_currency` and `spend_virtual_currency` as recommended game events ([Google](https://support.google.com/firebase/answer/6317498), HIGH). EVE Online publishes monthly charts of sinks and faucets, money supply and money velocity ([EVE Online, Nov 2024 report](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/monthly-economic-report-november-2024), LOW: chart names only, a PC game). Six early-warning metrics, kit recommendation (MEDIUM): net flow per currency per active player by cohort; median balance divided by the price of the next sink; share of players at cap; the largest faucet's share of grants; days to afford the next upgrade at median income; the distribution of balance left after purchases. Dashboards are in guide 12.

## Taxonomy

| Kind | Purpose | Earned by | Spent on | Bought | Cap or expiry | Tradable | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soft currency (coins) | Routine progress | Core loop, daily rewards, events | Upgrades, continues, boosters | Sometimes, as the one sold currency | Optional cap; no expiry | No | Inflation |
| Premium currency (gems) | Skips and exclusives | Purchase; a small free trickle | Skips, pulls, refills, exclusives | Yes | Purchased amount never expires | No | Obscured price, mismatched packs |
| Event currency | Gives one event its own economy | Event tasks | Event shop, grand prize | No (kit default) | Expires at the event end; leftover converts | No | Expiry feels like a loss |
| Social currency (friend points) | Rewards relationship actions | Gifts, support use, shared play | Low-tier pulls, gifts | No | Daily cap (kit default) | Gifted only | Pressure on friends; empty for solo players |
| Prestige or meta currency | Powers the reset loop | A reset, scaled by lifetime earnings | Permanent multipliers | No | None | No | A small multiplier makes the reset feel pointless |
| Energy, tickets, keys | Gate session volume or one activity | Time, gifts, rewards | One attempt or entry | Refills only | Regeneration stops at the cap | Gifted in some games | Read as "pay to skip" (guide 06) |
| Crafting materials | Long-run sink for surplus | Drops, stage clears, dismantling | Upgrades, crafting | Seldom, in bundles | Stack limit | No | One scarce material gates everything |
| XP and ranks | Mark progress, unlock content | Play | Never; thresholds only | Through boosters | None | No | Fast early, flat late (guide 08) |
| Boosters and consumables | Relief at a hard moment | Rewards, shop | Used in play | Yes | Timed ones end on the server clock | No | Sells power; stockpiles hide shortage |
| Equipment | Per-instance power and identity | Drops, crafting, packs | Upgraded, merged, sold | Via packs | Bag limit | Rare | Power creep devalues old pieces |
| Cosmetics | Self-expression, status | Shop, pass, events | Not spent after unlock | Yes | Permanent; limited sale windows | Rare | Time pressure to buy (guide 15) |
| Collectibles and units (cards, heroes, pets) and shards | Collection, team power | Packs, pulls, events | Upgrades, fusion, trade-ins | Via pulls with odds shown | Roster limit | No | Duplicates feel like a loss |
| Counters (trophies, stars) | Standing, unlocks | Play | Never spent | No | Seasonal reset in ranked play | No | Loss on defeat; farming |

The columns are the kit's synthesis (MEDIUM). The soft and premium split and the idle currencies follow Kolibri and Pecorella above; friend points follow a player guide to a gacha game ([Samurai Gamers](https://samurai-gamers.com/fate-grand-order/earning-friend-points/), LOW). Apple allows gifting of purchasable items, refundable only to the original purchaser and not exchangeable (Apple 3.1.1, HIGH), so any player-to-player transfer needs its own abuse and refund rules.

## Worksheet

One row per resource, filled before any reward is designed. Reasons use the ledger form `<block-slug>.<action>`; each becomes a `currency_flows` row, because a `grant` needs a faucet row and a `spend` a sink row (INV-7 of `billing.virtual-currency`). The two rows show the format.

| Name | Kind and code | Faucets by reason | Sinks by reason | Cap | Expiry | Bought | Shown on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coins | Soft currency, `coins` | `objectives.reward`, `daily-streak.claim` | `energy-lives.refill`, a game-owned booster purchase | None | None | No | Top bar, Shop |
| Gems | Premium currency, `gems` | `virtual-currency.pack-purchase`, `season-pass.tier-reward` | `energy-lives.refill` | None | Never (purchased) | Yes | Top bar, Shop |

## Default path

1. List every resource the design mentions, sort each by the storage classes of rule 1, and fill one worksheet row with its code, faucets and sinks by reason.
2. Cut the list to the currency set of rule 2 and write one sentence per remaining currency on why it exists. Demote the rest to items or counters.
3. Write the conversion, cap and expiry columns (rule 3), the price display (rule 4) and the pack ladder against the item price ladder (rule 5).
4. Write the reward ladder in minutes of play (rule 6), the first-session gift (rule 9) and the three presentation tiers (rule 7).
5. Give each item class a duplicate rule, an odds display where it is paid and random, and an inventory exit (rules 10 to 12). Take the odds floor from guide 06 and `15-manipulation-and-the-line.md`.
6. Simulate one week of median play per cohort in a spreadsheet, sum faucets and sinks per currency, and fix any currency whose net flow is positive with no sink (rule 8).
7. Compute the six early-warning metrics of rule 13 from ledger rows in the soft launch, and set thresholds from those numbers.
8. Place each resource on a screen from the maps above and send the list to `10-screens-and-navigation.md`.

## Gaps

- No source gives a recommended count of currencies or a measured cost per added currency. The published texts are a regulator's reading of price clarity and designers' opinions.
- No source gives reward amounts per step, level, session or day, or an experiment on reward timing in a mobile game. Supercell announced a chest test with instant and day-long chests at equal total value (guide 02), and no result was found.
- The juicy-design studies are short lab sessions on prototypes and measure experience, play time and performance. None measures retention or spend.
- Event-currency expiry rests on individual game rules (Elvenar, Kano Play). No study of player reaction was found.
- No source compares revenue of cosmetic-only and power-selling designs, and the sentiment evidence is a vendor survey and small studies. Hamari 2017 and Toccafondi 2026 were read as abstracts.
- No source measures inventory caps, timed boosters or crafting sinks. No ladder ratio for upgrade costs exists outside idle games, and the 4X rows rest on player guides.
- Where the real-money value of a currency-priced item comes from is open. CPC principle 1 asks for a value computed at the undiscounted rate, while `billing.virtual-currency` keeps the store's price string and derives no price (INV-16).
- Whether Apple's odds rule covers boxes bought with premium currency has no official answer in the sources found (guide 06 research notes, MEDIUM). The EU Digital Fairness Act text was unpublished on 2026-10-22.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every resource is a worksheet row with a code, a storage class, and faucets and sinks by ledger reason.
- [ ] The purchasable currencies are one soft and at most one premium, and each extra currency has a written reason.
- [ ] No purchase needs more than one currency exchange.
- [ ] Purchased currency never expires; event currency ends at the server `ends_at` and converts at a rate shown from the first day.
- [ ] Every price paid in currency has a real-money value the player can read, and an exact top-up exists for a shortfall.
- [ ] Each rung of the reward ladder lists its reward in minutes of play, and surprise bonuses are marked.
- [ ] Every reward animation is skippable and credits the balance at once.
- [ ] The first-session gift covers the first upgrade the tutorial shows.
- [ ] Every new faucet ships with a named sink or an offsetting cut.
- [ ] Each collectible type has a duplicate rule shown before the pull, and paid random items show odds before purchase.
- [ ] Every inventory cap has an exit, and every timer comes from the server clock.
- [ ] The six early-warning metrics are computed from the ledger with thresholds taken from the soft launch.
- [ ] Each resource has a screen in guide 10.
