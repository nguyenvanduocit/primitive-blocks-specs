# Manipulation and the Line

The techniques mobile games use to move a player toward spending or staying, each with how well it is evidenced, what the law and the stores say, and the honest alternative that serves the same goal. The guide is a recognition and decision tool: the owner sees each technique, knows its risk, and either takes the alternative or accepts the risk knowingly, in writing. It builds on guide 06 (`06-design-ethics-and-compliance.md`: the feature-to-tendency table, the three tests, the enforcement list) and adds the operational catalog, an alternative per technique and a pre-ship audit. It is not a method for getting more out of players.

Evidence: official pages (regulators, Apple, Google) read on 2026-10-27 and labelled HIGH; academic work named inline. In the catalog the label after "Evidence" grades how well a study shows that the technique changes behaviour: HIGH is a randomized field test of the technique in a game, MEDIUM is a randomized experiment outside games or in a lab, or a field result on a neighbouring lever, and LOW is correlation, qualitative work, analogy or no study found. The label after a status is confidence in the legal reading. Interviews 3 and 4 (`README.md`) decide how much of this applies.

## Decision rule

1. **Place the line with four tests, not with the mechanic.** Zagal, Björk and Lewis define a dark game pattern as one "used intentionally by a game creator to cause negative experiences for players that are against their best interests and happen without their consent" ([Zagal et al. 2013][zagal], HIGH, read in the paper). Susser, Roessler and Nissenbaum call manipulation "hidden influence", the covert subversion of someone's decision-making power ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3306006), MEDIUM, abstract). The EU consumer network says exploiting cognitive biases so that consumers overspend, or hold unneeded currency, is likely to unfairly distort their decisions ([CPC principles][kp], HIGH), and expects traders to adapt to the consumers a practice can actually reach, not only the target group (HIGH). The kit's tests: **autonomy** (could the player choose with their own interests in view), **transparency** (are the mechanism, odds and real cost shown before the tap), **reversibility** (can the choice be undone at a bearable cost: Google requires "an easy-to-use, online method to cancel" a subscription, [Google Play subscriptions][gsub], HIGH; the EU principles expect a fourteen-day withdrawal right, [CPC][kp], HIGH), **vulnerability** (who is reached). A hook that fails transparency or vulnerability is manipulation whatever its mechanism; one that passes all four is persuasion. This extends the three tests of guide 06 with reversibility (kit synthesis, MEDIUM).
2. **Read the status colour as a ship rule.** RED (prohibited or enforced against in a major market): not shipped. AMBER (permitted, under scrutiny): shipped only with the alternative or a signed entry (audit below). GREEN (fine with disclosure): shipped with the disclosure.
3. **Treat "it works" as the weakest argument.** Most evidence that these techniques change behaviour comes from shopping sites, lab tasks and surveys; game field tests are rare (catalog, Gaps). The one randomized field test on a game lever found that easier play cut purchases in the round but raised retention and long-run spending (guide 09 rule 6, [Ascarza et al.][ascarza], HIGH for one game), and a survey experiment found aggressive dark patterns "generated a powerful backlash" while mild ones did not ([Luguri and Strahilevitz][luguri], MEDIUM, not games).
4. **Assume children are reachable and the biggest spenders are vulnerable.** "Any video game which is not exclusively intended for an adult audience should expect a significant part of its player base to be under the age of 18", and games built on "whales" are judged by a stricter threshold ([CPC principle 7][kp], HIGH as the regulators' position). Interview answers lower the exposure; they do not remove the duty.
5. **Disclose first, then design.** Odds, real-money prices and subscription terms appear before the tap and in the store listing (guide 06 rule 1).
6. **Record every accepted risk.** An amber item has a name, a reason and a review date in the project contract.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Paid random rewards**: loot boxes, gacha pulls, chests opened with bought keys; variable-ratio reward and unpredictability | MEDIUM: online experiments found hidden odds plus selectively positive feedback raised willingness to pay by up to a hundred percent ([Cordes et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4349674), abstract); spending correlates with problem-gambling symptoms and the direction is unresolved (guide 06) | Minors; players with problem spending | RED for minors: no sales to under-sixteens without parental consent ([FTC order terms][cog], HIGH); loot boxes barred where minors have access in Brazil (guide 06). AMBER for adults with odds shown before purchase ([Apple 3.1.1][apple], [Google][gpay], HIGH) | Sell the named item at a fixed price; earn random drops by play (interview 3); if paid random stays, odds, pity counter and real-money price on one screen |
| **2. Near-miss presentation**: the reel stops one symbol short, the almost-rare pull animation | LOW: in a slot-machine task a near-miss raised the urge to play only when players felt in control ([Clark et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2929454)); no game spending test found | Gambling-prone players | AMBER, LOW: no rule found that names it; it sits inside the variable-reward scrutiny of the [joint statement][js] (HIGH) | Reveal the drawn result without an animation that implies a closer one |
| **3. Loss-framed streaks and expiring progress**: "your streak ends tonight"; loss avoidance, goal gradient | LOW: a language-app test that split the daily goal from the streak raised relative day-14 retention by 3.3 percent ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)); loss aversion is contested (guide 06) | Children; anxious or compulsive players | AMBER: the Commission's guidelines list streaks among features to disable by default for minors, voluntary and addressed to online platforms ([Commission, 14 July 2025](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors), HIGH) | A streak freeze earned by play; reward days played, not consecutive days; free repair |
| **4. Sunk-cost locks**: progress, rank or purchases lost on leaving; purchased currency that expires | LOW: people who paid more for theatre season tickets attended more plays over six months ([Arkes and Blumer 1985](https://cognition.aau.at/bg/BA/Arkes%20%26%20Blumer%201985.pdf), via a summary); transfer to games untested | Heavy spenders | RED where purchased currency expires ([Apple 3.1.1][apple], HIGH); AMBER elsewhere: the EU principles call terms that let a trader change currency value or block accounts without a way to contest unfair ([principle 6][kp], HIGH) | Purchased value never expires; an appeal path; season rewards claimable after the season ends |
| **5. Fake or resettable urgency timers**: a countdown that restarts, "ends tonight" on an offer that returns | MEDIUM, mixed: "must act now" messages did not raise purchase of a costly service ([Luguri][luguri]); an 11K-site crawl logged 393 countdown timers on 361 sites, one still valid after it expired ([Mathur et al. 2019][mathur], prevalence only); no game study | Children; hurried players | RED: UK banned practice 7 uses an in-game skin "only available for five days" that returns ten days later as its example, with fines up to the higher of £300,000 or ten percent of worldwide turnover ([CMA207][cma], HIGH); the EU network names "misleading countdown timers" ([joint statement][js], HIGH) | A server `ends_at` that never restarts; a recurring offer says it returns |
| **6. Artificial scarcity**: "only three left" or "limited run" on a digital item with no cap | LOW: no study found on scarcity claims for in-game items | Children | RED when false: [CMA207][cma] banned practice 20 (inaccurate information about availability, HIGH); "unfounded scarcity claims" named by the [joint statement][js] (HIGH) | State the true cap or schedule; list retired items with their return policy |
| **7. Price anchoring and decoys**: a crossed-out "value", a middle pack that makes the top pack look sensible | LOW for games: anchoring is long documented ([Furnham and Boo 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/soceco/v40y2011i1p35-42.html), record only); the decoy effect weakens outside numeric displays ([Frederick, Lee and Baskin 2014](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/6131/Limits%20of%20Attraction.pdf)); guide 03 found no game data | Credulous players; children | AMBER, MEDIUM: lawful when the reference price is real; the EU principles ask for the real-money price without quantity discounts ([principle 1][kp], HIGH) | Show the price per unit; claim "best value" only when it is true per unit |
| **8. Premium-currency bundles that obscure price and leave remainders**: pack sizes that never match item prices, several currencies, exchange chains | MEDIUM: in a randomized online experiment with 753 UK participants, willingness to pay for loot boxes was higher in virtual currency at 1:1 than in pounds ([Toccafondi et al. 2026](https://air.unipr.it/handle/11381/3056576), abstract) | Children; players with problem spending | RED: the FTC says children "don't fully understand the amount they're spending" and requires a direct US-dollar option ([FTC][cog], HIGH); principles 1 to 3 (real-money price, no mixed currencies, no forced remainders) are the public guidance the network says companies have not acted on; eleven coordinated actions followed on 30 September 2026 ([joint statement][js], HIGH; not findings of breach) | A real-money price beside every item; one premium currency; a pack or direct purchase for each exact price |
| **9. Discounts that are not discounts**: an inflated "was" price, a permanent "70 percent off" | LOW: no study found | Everyone | RED: a former price must be "the actual, bona fide price" offered "on a regular basis for a reasonably substantial period of time" ([FTC guides, 16 CFR 233.1][cfr233], HIGH; guides are interpretation, not statute) | Compare only with a price charged recently; call extras "bonus" |
| **10. Friction, then paid skip**: energy walls, build timers, wait gates tuned to sell | LOW: a 2022 heuristic evaluation names "Playing by Appointment" and "Pay to Skip" (guide 06); no source measures revenue from waits (guide 02) | Children; impatient players | AMBER: waiting is lawful; principle 3 targets design that forces more spend than needed ([CPC][kp], HIGH); Google's Families policy bars "emotionally manipulative tactics" to drive purchases ([Google][gfam], HIGH) | Waits that pay out; a free daily refill; price in money; no wall at the peak of investment |
| **11. Difficulty spikes tuned to sell boosts; undisclosed matchmaking or difficulty tuning** | HIGH that difficulty moves purchases: in a randomized trial on over three hundred thousand players for twelve weeks, easier play cut purchases in the round and raised retention and long-run spending ([Ascarza et al.][ascarza]); matchmaking that minimizes churn risk is a published method ([EOMM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.06820)); Activision called its patent on matchmaking to drive purchases exploratory and not in any game ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/activision-clarifies-its-patent-for-encouraging-mi/1100-6454147), MEDIUM) | Everyone; children | AMBER, LOW: no rule found on difficulty tuning or matchmaking disclosure | Visible help (hint, skip, extra moves); easing only, capped, written in the contract (guide 09); never match players to sell items |
| **12. Personalized pricing and spender-tier targeting**: price, offer size or wait length set by predicted spend | LOW: vendors call personalized offers a lever and no independent lift exists (guide 03) | Players with problem spending | AMBER, rising: the FTC proposed a policy statement on 19 August 2026 saying undisclosed use of personal data for personalized pricing "could violate the FTC Act" ([FTC][ftcpp], HIGH for the proposal; final status not found); the EU principles judge "whale" business models by a stricter threshold ([CPC][kp], HIGH) | One price per item per market; offers by lifecycle (first purchase), not wallet; disclose any personalization |
| **13. Social-obligation pressure**: clan donation quotas, public contribution boards, gifts that must be returned, invites that unlock rewards | LOW: listed as a "social pyramid scheme" pattern ([Zagal et al.][zagal]); no effect size | Children; anxious players | AMBER; RED toward children: do not "persuade adults to buy it for them" ([CPC principle 7][kp], HIGH); UK banned practice 30 bars direct appeals to children to buy or to persuade parents, with a mobile-game example ([CMA207][cma], HIGH) | Opt-in gifting of free items; contribution without shaming; no penalty for leaving |
| **14. Notification guilt**: "your streak dies tonight", a distressed mascot | LOW: no controlled study found; vendor advice only (guide 09) | Children; anxious players | AMBER, LOW: the Commission's guidelines list push notifications among features to disable by default for minors (voluntary); UK banned practice 28 covers persistent unwanted solicitations ([CMA207][cma]), and no source applies it to game push | A benefit-led message, a frequency cap, quiet hours, per-category toggles (`messaging.push-reengagement`) |
| **15. Hidden or tiny close buttons, confirm-shaming**: a faint X, "No, I don't want rewards" | MEDIUM: hidden information, trick questions and obstruction were the most effective tactics in a controlled test ([Luguri][luguri], not games); the crawl counted 169 confirm-shaming instances ([Mathur][mathur]) | Everyone; children | RED for ads: Google bars a "false dismiss button" and full-screen interstitials not closeable after fifteen seconds ([Ads policy][gads], HIGH); Epic paid $245 million in refunds over dark patterns that led to charges on the press of a single button ([FTC][epic], HIGH). AMBER for shaming copy: no rule found | Accept and decline of equal weight; a close control with a full hit area; "Not now" |
| **16. Auto-renew and trial failures**: grey price, a silent trial conversion, a buried cancel | MEDIUM: mild dark patterns more than doubled sign-up for a dubious service and aggressive ones nearly quadrupled it ([Luguri][luguri], not games) | Trial users; children | RED: Google requires terms, price, billing frequency and an easy cancel ([policy][gsub], HIGH); Apple 3.1.2(c) ([Apple][apple], HIGH); Amazon paid a $1 billion penalty and $1.5 billion in refunds over Prime enrollment and cancellation, not a game ([FTC, 25 September 2025][amazon], HIGH) | One pre-purchase screen with price, period, trial length, price after the trial and renewal date; cancel in settings; a reminder before conversion (`billing.subscriptions`) |
| **17. Designs aimed at children**: cartoon IP, direct exhortation to buy, asking adults to pay | LOW: Ofcom's 2025 qualitative work with parents and children links persuasive features to financial harm ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/safety-technology/research-into-persuasive-design-features-and-potential-child-financial-harms)); the EU network holds children more susceptible to currency obscuring ([principle 7][kp]) | Under-sixteens, under-eighteens | RED: direct exhortation to children is "an explicit prohibition in EU consumer protection law" ([joint statement][js], HIGH); Apple Kids Category allows purchases only behind a parental gate (1.3, [Apple][apple], HIGH); Google Families bars manipulative tactics and an unclear line between coins and money ([Google][gfam], HIGH); Epic paid a $275 million COPPA penalty ([FTC][epic], HIGH) | No shop surface for a child profile, or a parental gate with real-money spend off by default; use the platform age signal |
| **18. Always-on continuation cues**: autoplay of the next round, "one more", no stopping point, hidden clocks | LOW: no controlled game study; guide 09 lists the cues to audit | Children; compulsive players | AMBER, MEDIUM: the Commission's guidelines list autoplay and streaks among features to disable by default for minors (voluntary, HIGH); time limits for minors exist in some markets (guide 06) | Natural stopping points, a session summary, an optional break reminder, no auto-start |

## Enforcement as it stands

- **United States.** Epic Games, 19 December 2022: $245 million in refunds for dark patterns that "led players to incur unwanted charges based on the press of a single button", plus a $275 million COPPA penalty ([FTC][epic], HIGH). Cognosphere (Genshin Impact), 17 January 2025: a proposed settlement with a $20 million penalty, no loot boxes to under-sixteens without parental consent, truthful odds and cost, a direct dollar option; the FTC said a federal judge must approve it and entry of the order was not confirmed ([FTC][cog], HIGH for the terms). Apple, 15 January 2014: refunds of at least $32.5 million for children's unauthorized in-app charges ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/47265), HIGH). The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 and the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act is still enforced ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-07-11-click-to-cancel-just-got-cancelled-eighth-circuit-vacates-entirety-of-ftcs-negative-option-rule), MEDIUM).
- **European Union.** The consumer network published seven non-exhaustive principles on in-game virtual currencies on 21 March 2025 ([CPC][kp], HIGH). On 30 September 2026 it announced eleven coordinated actions against ten named companies, saying a high number of companies "have not made any substantive changes" after the guidance; failure to address the concerns may lead to national enforcement, and the actions are not findings of breach ([joint statement][js], HIGH). The Commission's guidelines under Article 28 of the Digital Services Act (14 July 2025) are voluntary, serve as the benchmark for that article and exclude micro and small enterprises (HIGH); whether a given game is an online platform under the Act was not settled by the sources.
- **United Kingdom.** The unfair-trading regime of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act applies from 6 April 2025, with the CMA's guidance of 18 November 2025 ([CMA207][cma], HIGH). The advertising regulator's notice requires app store listings to say whether a game contains loot boxes; "Offers In-App Purchases" is not enough, and monitoring runs from 26 May 2026 ([ASA notice][asa], HIGH). The industry's voluntary principles (July 2023) ask for parental controls against under-eighteens acquiring loot boxes without permission and lenient refunds for unauthorized child spend ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2023/07/24/new-uk-principles-and-guidance-on-loot-boxes-published-102ik8a), MEDIUM). The subscription regime starts in January 2027 ([Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/commercial-contracts-and-outsourcing/the-dmcca-subscription-contracts-the-timeline-just-got-shorter/)) or spring 2027 ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/04/subscription-contracts)); sources conflict (LOW).
- **Loot-box rulings.** Belgium: the Gaming Commission found paid loot boxes in three games illegal gambling in April 2018 ([iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/casino-games/belgium-rules-video-game-loot-boxes-breach-gambling-laws/), MEDIUM); on 16 January 2025 the Antwerp Enterprise Court held Apple in breach of Belgian gambling law for offering a game's loot boxes and referred a question to the EU Court of Justice ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/03/an-iphone-a-gambling-problem-and-the-loot-box-debate), MEDIUM, summary only). Netherlands: in March 2022 the Council of State overturned the gambling authority's fine against Electronic Arts, treating the packs as part of a skill game ([Lottery Daily](https://lotterydaily.com/2022/03/11/news/ksa-overruled-on-fifa-loot-box-charges/), MEDIUM). The record is mixed and turns on national law and facts. Brazil and South Korea are in guide 06 and were not rechecked.
- **Stores.** Apple (the page shows no date): odds before purchase, purchased currency "may not expire" (3.1.1), subscription terms (3.1.2), purchases behind a parental gate in the Kids Category (1.3), and apps "should never prey on users" or "trick them into making unwanted purchases", with repeated manipulative behaviour leading to removal from the developer program (5.6) ([Apple][apple], HIGH). Google Play: odds "in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to" the purchase ([Payments][gpay], HIGH), plus the subscription, ads and Families policies cited above; Families reserves the right to remove apps for "overly aggressive commercial tactics".

## Vulnerable players

- **Platform controls that exist.** Apple Ask to Buy is on by default and cannot be turned off for a child under eighteen in some states, countries and regions ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105055), HIGH). Google Play lets a parent require approval for all content, only in-app purchases, or none, and only for purchases through Google Play billing ([Google](https://support.google.com/families/answer/7039872), HIGH). Apple's Declared Age Range API and Google's Play Age Signals API (beta) share an age category where the law requires it, for example in Brazil and some US states ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/age-assurance/), [Google](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/use-age-signals-api), MEDIUM, regional and in beta). Google refunds in-app purchases within forty-eight hours, after which the developer decides ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/15574908), HIGH). Neither platform page read above offers a monthly spend cap or a cool-off.
- **What the game adds (kit recommendation, LOW: no source tests whether these reduce harm).** Real-money spending off by default for any profile not verified as an adult, which the EU principles list as an action point for games not limited to adults ([CPC principle 7][kp], HIGH); the platform controls and the age signal wired in; a player-set spend limit that cannot be raised or removed until a cool-off has passed; a purchase confirmation that shows the real-money total; a lenient refund path for spend a guardian did not authorize. The limit and the cool-off length are the owner's to set, and no source gives a safe number. Age gates and parental controls "do not limit traders' responsibilities" ([CPC][kp], HIGH).
- **What the harm research says and does not say.** Loot box spending and problem-gambling symptoms correlate at a small to moderate size and the direction of cause is unresolved (guide 06). The two experiments above measure willingness to pay in online tasks, not harm. Ofcom's study is qualitative. The EU network cites a "broad consensus among academics" ([joint statement][js]), a regulator's summary and not a measurement. Spending data in these studies is mostly self-reported.

## Business case

- **Refund and fine exposure.** Epic $245 million in refunds and $275 million in penalties; Apple at least $32.5 million; Cognosphere $20 million; Nexon 11.6 billion won (guide 06); UK penalties up to the higher of £300,000 or ten percent of worldwide turnover ([CMA207][cma]). Each game case turned on deception or children, not on engagement mechanics (guide 06).
- **Store review.** Apple states that repeated manipulative behaviour leads to removal from its developer program (5.6), and Google's Families policy reserves removal for overly aggressive tactics (HIGH). No public figure on rejections or removals was found.
- **Chargebacks, ratings and reviews.** No source quantifies chargeback rates or rating damage from monetization tactics. A study of user-generated data on 1,496 games found dark patterns in games thought benign, which shows players write about them and not what that costs ([Niknejad et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05039), MEDIUM).
- **Long-term revenue.** No cohort-level study of aggressive tactics in games was found. The nearest evidence is the difficulty trial and the backlash experiment in rule 3, which together suggest that tuning every wall for the highest immediate sale is not the revenue-maximizing choice; that reading is an inference (LOW).

## Pre-ship audit

Answer each question pass or fail with evidence (a screen, a config value, a line of copy). R: a fail blocks release. A: a fail needs the alternative or a signed entry.

| ID | Question, asked of every feature | Class |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | Does the player see the true cost in real money before the tap, beside any currency price? | R |
| U2 | Can the player decline or close with a control as visible as accept, with no penalty and no shaming copy? | A |
| U3 | Is every deadline and scarcity claim true: a server `ends_at`, a real cap, no restart? | R |
| U4 | Is the odds table visible before any paid random reward, in the listing and in the game? | R |
| U5 | Could a child reach this: real-money spend off by default, no direct exhortation, no request to ask an adult to buy? | R |
| U6 | Can the purchase be undone: store refund path, in-app cancel, withdrawal right in the EU, no expiring purchased currency? | R |

| Feature (block) | Extra questions |
|---|---|
| Daily reward and streak (`engagement.daily-streak`) | S1 A missed day is recoverable without paying, or the paid repair is optional and priced in money (A). S2 Reminder copy names a benefit, not a loss (A). S3 A lost streak never removes purchased items (R) |
| Energy (`engagement.energy-lives`) | E1 The player can always continue without paying and the wait is stated (A). E2 The refill price is in money or an exact currency amount, with no forced remainder (A). E3 Wait length and price do not vary with spend history unless disclosed (A) |
| Season pass (`engagement.season-pass`) | P1 Both reward tracks are listed in full before purchase (R). P2 Any "worth X" figure is computed from prices actually charged (R). P3 The fate of unfinished tiers at season end is stated before purchase (A) |
| Events (`engagement.event-calendar`) | V1 A repeating event says it repeats (R). V2 Rewards depend on play, not on spend alone (A) |
| Objectives (`engagement.objectives`) | O1 No objective requires a purchase or an invitation to buy (R). O2 A missed objective costs no earned progress (A). O3 Gifts and invites are optional and a free path to the reward exists (A) |
| Currency packs (`billing.virtual-currency`) | C1 One premium currency and no chained exchanges (A). C2 A pack or direct purchase covers each item's exact price (A). C3 Purchased currency never expires (R). C4 A player-set spend limit whose raise waits out a cool-off, and a confirmation with the real-money total, exist (A). C5 Odds sit next to any random item (R) |
| Subscriptions (`billing.subscriptions`) | B1 One screen shows price, period, trial length, price after the trial and renewal date before purchase (R). B2 Cancel is reachable in the app or by a link to store subscription management (R). B3 A reminder precedes trial conversion (A) |
| Push (`messaging.push-reengagement`) | N1 Each message names a visible benefit, with no guilt or loss copy (A). N2 A frequency cap, quiet hours and per-category toggles exist (A). N3 For any profile that may be a minor, push re-engagement stays off until someone opts in (A) |

Scoring: a feature is BLOCKED if any R fails, CONDITIONAL if only A items fail, PASS otherwise; the game takes its worst feature. Decision rule for amber (kit recommendation): the owner who answered interviews 3 and 4 signs, with a second reviewer who did not build the feature. The entry goes in the project contract with the question id and catalog row, the choice (alternative or accept) and its reason, the evidence grade relied on, the market and audience, both signers, the date and a review-by date. An amber item that a minor can reach takes the alternative, because the EU principles judge a practice by who it reaches. Entries reopen when interview 3 or 4 changes, when a market is added and when the legal snapshot changes.

## Default path

1. Answer interviews 3 and 4 and write them into the project contract.
2. List the features the game ships (guide 11, `11-feature-specs.md`) and mark each catalog row that appears.
3. For each marked row take the alternative; where the team keeps the technique, open an amber entry.
4. Run the audit on the screen drafts (guide 10, `10-screens-and-navigation.md`) before build and on the built flows before release. Fix every R.
5. Add the shop controls (a spend limit with a cool-off on raising it, a purchase confirmation) and wire the platform age signal.
6. Recheck the legal snapshot in the month before launch and after any EU or UK announcement (guide 06 rule 4).
7. Rerun the audit when an offer, a timer or a line of copy changes. Rewards and resources are in guide 14 (`14-rewards-resources-and-items.md`).

## Gaps

- No game study measures the long-run revenue effect of aggressive tactics other than difficulty; no source quantifies chargebacks or rating damage.
- No game field test was found for timers, scarcity, anchoring, streak copy, notification guilt, social pressure or continuation cues; those grades rest on shopping-site, lab and survey work or on nothing.
- Not opened: the Belgian and Dutch judgments (secondary reports only), the OECD definition of dark commercial patterns, the Digital Services Act text on the Official Journal page, and the full texts of Cordes et al. and Toccafondi et al. (abstracts only). The Commission's press page for the 30 September 2026 action did not load; the date comes from the network's joint statement.
- Open legal points: whether the Commission's minors guidelines reach a given game, the start date of the UK subscription regime, entry of the Cognosphere order, the final FTC statement on personalized pricing. Australia, Japan, China and Vietnam were not rechecked here.
- No source tests whether spend limits, cool-offs or purchase confirmations reduce harm or revenue.
