# Keeping Players

A player stays when a session ends on a result they chose, when they hold something they built, when the middle game still has somewhere to go, and, after they slip away, when the way back is easy and honest. Guide 02 lists the outside reasons to return (daily, weekly, seasonal) and guide 07 shapes the hook and the session. This guide covers the rest: how a session ends, which investments hold a player for weeks, why the middle game stalls, which signals show a player slipping, and how to win one back.

Evidence: a randomized difficulty trial on a Candy Crush-style game (Ascarza, Netzer and Runge), a CHI PLAY study on Angry Birds Dream Blast, a 2014 Wooga win-back test, Duolingo's growth account, platform docs, and vendor and practitioner guides, named inline with HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW. Each measured study covers one game or one app, and no source gives a win-back benchmark (see Gaps).

## Decision rule

1. **End each session where the player chooses to stop.** An expert statement without an experiment says many video games lack stopping cues because they are "built to be endless or have long-range goals that we don't like to abandon" ([Alter, quoted by NYU Stern, 2017](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/news/professor-adam-alter-comments-differences-between-television-and-modern-video-games), MEDIUM). The kit's reading is a result screen that shows what was won and what comes next, the designed stopping point of guide 07 (kit recommendation, MEDIUM). A stop the player did not choose costs more than it looks: in one game's data, players stopped playing more often right after clearing a gate level (a five-day wait unless coins pay), even with lives left, and gate levels carried more churn than other levels ([Ascarza, Netzer and Runge, working paper, Dec 2023](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/citation_file_upload/ascarza_netzer_runge_2023-12-04.pdf), HIGH as reported, model-free charts from one game). A timer stop needs a stated purpose and a way to continue without paying (kit recommendation). `engagement.energy-lives` makes waiting a complete path and states the exact time on the empty-energy sheet (INV-15). `engagement.timed-queues` gives the wait a job: the player starts a build, craft or research, leaves, and collects later, with a free path, a free finish for a short remainder (five minutes by default) and no countdown that restarts or grows. A countdown with no job behind it exists only to sell and fails test (a) of guide 06.
2. **Offer "one more round" and never manufacture it.** The pull into the next round (guide 07 rule 3) becomes compulsion when the game builds the urge: a near-miss the player's skill did not produce, auto-advance with no result screen, a countdown on the continue prompt, a reward for an unbroken run of rounds. These are kit judgement (MEDIUM). A near-miss raises the urge to keep playing when the player feels in control, and unfinished tasks pull players back (guide 06), so both are levers to use with care; paid near-misses and pressure aimed at minors sit at the exploitative end (guide 06, guide 07 rule 3). Kit rule: the continue prompt appears after the result screen, has no timer, and leaving takes one tap. Run the three tests of guide 06 on every continuation cue.
3. **Hold the player with at most two investments, matched to the archetype.** An investment is something the player built or became that leaving would lose. Choose from the table below by the answer to interview 1. No source measures a retention lift for any of them.
4. **Plan the middle game before launch: runway, walls, economy.** Nielsen found new-content demand peaked three to five weeks after launch for the big-bang titles it studied, and put the plateau down to early adopters running out of content or tiring of it ([Nielsen, 2016](https://www.nielsen.com/th/insights/2016/long-term-mobile-game-success-beyond-awareness-and-adoption), LOW: old, titles and sample not stated). Roblox's docs say that without live updates even dedicated players lose interest, with a cadence from weekly to monthly by team capacity ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/liveops-essentials), MEDIUM, platform-specific); a thesis on one small game found six small releases raised retention "but not by much" ([Koski, 2019](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/64353), LOW as effect evidence). Difficulty walls are measurable: in Angry Birds Dream Blast (95,266 players, churn defined as seven days without play) a level's pass rate tracks churn weakly across all players (Spearman r of -0.144) and strongly among players who quit without completing it (-0.586) ([Roohi et al., CHI PLAY 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12937), HIGH as reported, one game); King's level-65 account is in guide 07 rule 7. As content and rewards grow, review faucets and sinks each season (guide 03 rule 3); Royal Match cut level-completion coin rewards by roughly 30% to absorb the endless album's extra reward source ([Naavik, Nov 2025](https://naavik.co/digest/live-ops-trends-powering-mobile-puzzle/), HIGH). Write the runway into the contract: units shipped, units per release, and the date the fastest cohort runs out. Goals past the first month belong to guide 08; `engagement.objectives` carries them in `seasonal` and `lifetime` scopes (the lifetime scope never resets) and closes the "now what?" gap after the tutorial with a `starter` scope.
5. **Define churn from your own session gaps and watch four signals.** Studies draw the line differently: seven days without play (Roohi), fourteen days without a login for high-value players ([Runge et al., 2014, via a Game Developer summary](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/churn-prediction-and-prevention-in-high-value-players), MEDIUM, paper not opened), thirty days in the gate analysis of rule 1. No source gives a threshold to copy. Kit recommendation: take the window from the soft launch, at the gap that most returning sessions fall under. The signals table lists what to log; guide 12 defines the event envelope.
6. **Help the player who is slipping, and say what the help is.** In a randomized trial on about 330,000 players, those past level 20 who played fewer than twenty rounds in the past week were split in half; one half got a more favorable piece allocation on the board, larger for players who had played less. It raised the share who played the next day by 2.7 percentage points (control rate 29.5%) and the share who played within the next seven days by about 2.5 points (control 64.3%), and long-run purchases rose even though purchases per round fell (Ascarza et al., HIGH as reported; one puzzle game, one firm; the paper does not say whether players were told). Kit recommendation: ship visible help first (a hint, a skip, extra moves offered after repeated failure), because a visible offer answers test (a) of guide 06 directly. If the team ships invisible easing, it only ever eases, it is written into the contract, and it is capped. For goals, `engagement.objectives` has an optional scaling rule, off by default, that sizes a target from the player's recent activity within bounds, scales the reward by the same factor and never reads spending (INV-17).
7. **Keep pressure out of the response.** Guilt copy: a push vendor advises leading with what is new or waiting and avoiding "we miss you" ([Countly](https://countly.com/blog/how-to-use-push-notifications-to-bring-lapsed-players-back-to-your-game), LOW). False urgency: EU consumer authorities list time-limited offers as a pressure tactic (guide 06, MEDIUM), and the FTC's Epic settlement of $245 million in refunds turned on dark patterns in buttons and charges (guide 06, HIGH). Hidden tuning: Nexon Korea was fined 11.6 billion won for lowering gacha drop rates without telling players (guide 06, MEDIUM). No source shows hidden per-player tuning to force spend being shipped. King's level-65 test (a harder version lifted conversion in the short term while easier versions kept players longer, guide 07 rule 7, MEDIUM) and the randomized trial of rule 6 both favor easing for retention (MEDIUM).
8. **Receive a returning player with orientation and a fair gift.** Top games ship four patterns: welcome-back gifts, return offers sold as shortcuts, return missions and buffs, and friend recall that pays both sides ([GameRefinery, 2021](https://gamerefinery.com/four-ways-how-mobile-games-re-engage-lapsed-players), MEDIUM that they ship, no effect data). A free-currency gift sent after fourteen days of absence did not significantly cut churn against a no-action control in Wooga's test (rule 9), so pair a gift with a reason to play: new content, a short mission ladder, a progress boost. A push vendor advises scaling the reward to the player's value so the economy is not devalued (Countly, LOW). Duolingo tracks "resurrected" users, whose first activity follows thirty or more days away, as their own retention rate ([Lenny's Newsletter, Duolingo growth account](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth), MEDIUM: one app, the company's own account). Kit rules: the first screen after return shows what changed and what waits and carries no countdown; the comeback reward is one-time and smaller than what an active player earns over the same stretch, so staying away is never the better play (LOW). Blocks cover parts of the flow. `messaging.push-reengagement` grants one comeback reward per lapse and per cooldown on return, whether or not notifications are on (INV-15); its welcome-back sheet states what was added with no deadline and no streak-loss framing, and the orientation (what changed, what waits) stays the game's. `engagement.objectives` assigns a returning player only the current window, with no backlog (INV-15). `engagement.timed-queues` offers an empty slot a dismissible start-something card, at most twice a day per queue and once per app open, and stops after three dismissals in a row (INV-20). For a guest, `auth.guest-account` tells the returning player whether the account was reactivated or purged, and purges only after a year idle (a kit default), with paid holdings exempt by default.
9. **Climb the ladder below and test every rung against a holdout.** Delivery, consent and caps live in `messaging.push-reengagement`. Wooga split high-value players 40%, 40% and 20% into win-back, predictive and no-action groups; predictive outreach drew about five times the email clicks of win-back outreach (10.95% versus 2.16%) and churn still did not differ significantly from the control (p of 0.41), so clicks are not the metric ([Game Developer summary of Runge et al., 2014](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/churn-prediction-and-prevention-in-high-value-players), MEDIUM). A standing holdout is typically one to five percent of users ([Spotify Confidence](https://confidence.spotify.com/glossary/holdout-group), MEDIUM, vendor docs), and one marketing guide's lapsed-subscriber example holds out ten percent ([ATTN](https://www.attnagency.com/blog/holdout-testing-marketing-guide), LOW). `messaging.push-reengagement` ships the split: by default a stable five percent of profiles, assigned once and withheld from comeback messages, decided last so both groups are made of messages that would otherwise have gone out. Both groups get the same comeback reward on return, so the comparison isolates the notification, and it reads `returned` inside a window (INV-8, INV-19); the block specifies the measure, not a significance test or a sample size. Kit recommendation: read the share who return and still play a week later from your own analytics (guide 12), and give the reward its own split to test the reward.

## Investment mechanisms

Cost and fit are this kit's judgement (MEDIUM).

| Mechanism | What the player holds | Evidence | Cost to build | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collection or album | A set to complete | Endless albums are common in top casual puzzle and were built to remove the content ceiling ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/live-ops-trends-powering-mobile-puzzle/), HIGH); completion is one of Quantic Foundry's player motivations (guide 06, MEDIUM); no effect size | High: content plus a perpetual reward source to balance; `engagement.items` stores collectibles and decides what a duplicate becomes (convert, trade in or cap) | Casual puzzle, character-collector RPG; idle uses prestige (guide 02) |
| Customization | A look that is theirs (avatar, base, deck) | Choosing cosmetics raised identification with the avatar in a 24-person lab study, with no change in fun or performance ([Böffel et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.770139/full), LOW as retention evidence); self-made things are valued more when finished ([Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/12136084), HIGH for the finding, untested in games); teaching it early is common advice ([Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/optimizing-ftue/), MEDIUM, no effect size) | Low to medium: art, little economy; `engagement.items` stores cosmetics as equippable instances | All four; strongest where the player builds something visible |
| Owned items and upgrades | A hero, tool or gear piece the player raised | Self-made things are valued more when finished (Norton et al., above; HIGH for the finding, untested in games); no source measures retention from upgrades | Medium to high: `engagement.items` specifies per-copy levels, an upgrade that pays materials and raises the level as one unit, bag limits that hold a reward until there is room, and expiry | Character-collector RPG, 4X, idle |
| Visible, portable progress | A level, map or roster that survives a new phone | Progress made today went with playing tomorrow in one game's data (Ascarza et al., HIGH as reported, a correlation); goal gradient and endowed progress (guide 06); no source measures churn after a lost device | Low to show; accounts and a backend to carry: `auth.guest-account` lets a player start with no sign-up and link Google or Apple later on the same account, so progress survives a lost phone when the link or a recovery code came first; `data.progress-sync` carries the progress | All; matters most where progress took weeks |
| Social ties | Someone who notices an absence | No trustworthy multiplier; a thesis found a day-30 link in one of two games (guide 04, MEDIUM) | Medium to high: moderation, cold start; `ugc.friends-leaderboard` for the asynchronous start | RPG and 4X; depth per guide 04 |
| Identity, status and mastery | A rank, title or skill others can see | Competence, relatedness and autonomy each predicted enjoyment and future play ([Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf), HIGH); clans lift cosmetic and VIP sales ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/), MEDIUM: opinion) | Medium: ranks, titles, boards; curves in guide 08 | Competitive RPG and 4X; casual puzzle through leaderboard events |

## Churn signals

| Signal | What to log | Evidence | Respectful response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Widening session gaps | Days since the last session, and rounds in the past week against the player's own norm | The trial's trigger was a past-level-20 player under twenty rounds in a week (Ascarza et al., HIGH as reported); Wooga's predictors were time series of logins, rounds, level and balances (MEDIUM) | Ease the next round with visible help; hold outbound messages until the first lapse band |
| Stalled progression | Rounds and play-days on the current level, wins in the last day | The same trial's "stuck" fields; players stop after a gate (HIGH as reported) | A hint, a skip or a short boost; review the level or gate for everyone |
| Failed-level streaks | Attempts per level, and quitters per level who never completed it | Quitters without completion track pass rate (Roohi et al., HIGH as reported) | Rework the level; one visible offer after repeated failure |
| Resource exhaustion | Empty lives or energy, an empty soft currency at a blocked moment, a session that ends at the block | Gate stops above; a 2022 heuristic evaluation labels timers "Playing by Appointment" and "Pay to Skip" ([Dahlan and Susanty, deceptive.design, 28 Nov 2022](https://www.deceptive.design/articles/finding-dark-patterns-in-casual-mobile-games-using-heuristic-evaluation), MEDIUM) | A refill earned by play, or an event window of free lives (Royal Match grants unlimited lives as an event reward, guide 02) |

## Comeback ladder

| Days since the last session | Player state | What the game does | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Still opening, below their own rhythm | Slipping | In-game help from rule 6; no outbound message | Trial trigger was a weekly activity dip (Ascarza et al., HIGH as reported) |
| About three to fourteen, set by game depth | First lapse | One message tied to something real (a waiting reward, a finished timer, new content) | First push at three to seven days for casual and seven to fourteen for deeper games (Countly, LOW) |
| Up to about thirty | Lapsed | A few spaced messages and a new reason (event, update); stop on opt-out | Three to five pushes over thirty to sixty days, at least five to seven days apart (Countly, LOW) |
| Beyond about thirty | Dormant | Stop pushing; the welcome-back flow of rule 8 waits in the game | Duolingo's resurrected-user definition (MEDIUM) |

The bands are scale only; set the cut points from the churn window of rule 5. `messaging.push-reengagement` configures the ladder as steps keyed to days since the last session. Its defaults are design choices, not sourced: `lapse-d3` (reminder), `lapse-d7` (gift), `lapse-d14` (update) and `lapse-d28` (final), a run that ends at day thirty or after six unopened sends, and caps of two a day and eight a week.

## Retention problem to first hypothesis

| Problem | First hypothesis to check | Lever to try | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drop at day 1 | Fun arrives late or friction comes before play (guide 01, MEDIUM; no source ranks the causes) | Read the tutorial funnel for the step with the biggest loss; remove the earliest ask | Guides 01, 05 |
| Drop at day 7 | The tutorial's goals ran out and no return reason took over; one practitioner puts the meta layer as the main reason to relaunch by days three to five ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/), MEDIUM, judgment seen as a search snippet) | Check that days two to six hand out a next goal and a return reason; add the daily layer | Guides 02, 07, 08; `engagement.daily-streak` |
| Drop at day 30 | A wall: churners cluster at one level, gate or empty resource (rules 1, 4, 6) | Segment churners by last level reached; rework the level or soften the gate; compare the runway with the fastest cohort | This guide; guide 07 rule 11; `engagement.energy-lives` |
| Drop after a purchase | No source. First check that the purchase delivered and felt worth it (refunds, support contacts), then whether it carried the player past the content or goal they had (inference, LOW) | Show the next goal right after the purchase; compare buyers and non-buyers by cohort on return in the next week | Guide 03; `billing.in-app-purchase` |
| Drop after an event ends | Boom and bust: a finite event ends and no goal remains, the ceiling endless albums remove (Naavik, HIGH); back-to-back high-intensity events can burn players out ([Moran, 2021](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/liveops-essentials-part-2-the-cadence), LOW) | An always-on goal beside events, staggered ends, a bridge reward into the next event | Guide 02; `engagement.event-calendar` |

## Default path

1. Take the answer to interview 1 and pick at most two investments from the table.
2. Draw the session end screen and list every continuation cue; run the three tests of guide 06 on each (worksheet 1 and 2).
3. Write the content runway, and after the soft launch mark the walls: levels with the most quitters and the lowest pass rate (worksheet 4 and 5).
4. Set the churn window and the four signals from soft-launch gaps, and fire them as events (guide 12).
5. Build the help ladder with visible help first; any invisible easing only eases, is capped and sits in the contract.
6. Build the welcome-back screen before the first outbound message; add `messaging.push-reengagement` once the notification soft ask works (guide 01).
7. Start the lapsed program with a holdout from the first cohort and read it on return-and-still-playing.
8. When a drop appears, walk the problem table before changing anything.

## Worksheet

Copy these into the project contract and answer each in one or two lines.

Session
1. Which screen ends each session, what does it show (result, reward, next goal), and which gates or empty resources also end it?
2. Which continuation cues exist (next-level button, rewarded continue, double-or-nothing), and which test of guide 06 does each answer?

Investment and middle game
3. Which one or two investments, carried by which block or guide, and what do they cost in content and economy?
4. How many content units ship at launch and per release, and on what date does the fastest cohort run out?
5. Which levels or gates are known walls from the soft launch?

Churn and help
6. What is the churn window, and which soft-launch gap distribution set it?
7. Which thresholds on rounds, play-days or wins trigger which help, and is the help visible to the player?

Comeback and measurement
8. Where do the ladder bands start, what is the comeback reward, and why is it smaller than active play earns?
9. What does the welcome-back screen show, and which counters and offers does it hold back?
10. What is the holdout share and duration, who reads it, and is the primary metric return-and-still-playing?
11. Which answer fails a guide 06 test, and what changes?

## Gaps

- No benchmark for comeback-reward reactivation. The only randomized win-back test found (2014, high-value players, free currency, one of two casual games) was not significant and is known through a blogger's summary; the paper was not opened.
- No source measures the retention lift of any investment. The customization evidence is a 24-person lab study of identification, the collection evidence is prevalence, and the self-made-value finding is untested in games.
- The difficulty trial is one game and one firm, and the December 2023 working paper was read; a later journal version was not opened. No source says how players react when told about easing, or shows any mobile game tuning an individual player's difficulty to sell.
- The Roohi study is a correlation on one game. No threshold for stall signals (rounds on a level, failed-attempt streak) survived sourcing, and churn windows differ from study to study.
- Content cadence against retention has one old Nielsen note and one thesis; there is no cadence-to-retention curve.
- Nothing was found on churn after a purchase or on returning-player needs by genre; absence-tolerant design (resources that accrue while the player is away) appeared only as one search snippet about a single game. Duolingo's evidence is an education app's own account.
- No source gives the return-time distribution of lapsed mobile players, so the guest purge defaults of `auth.guest-account` and the ladder defaults of `messaging.push-reengagement` are design choices; the holdout in the latter has no significance test or sample size.
- Store policy for re-engagement messages was not rechecked here; it belongs to `messaging.push-reengagement`.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every session ends on a result screen, and every gate or empty-resource stop has a purpose and an earnable refill.
- [ ] Each continuation cue has no timer, follows a result screen and passed the three tests of guide 06.
- [ ] No more than two investments are chosen, each matched to the archetype.
- [ ] The content runway is written down with the date the fastest cohort runs out.
- [ ] The churn window comes from soft-launch gaps, and the four signals are logged.
- [ ] Help for a slipping player is visible, or invisible easing only eases and is capped.
- [ ] No message or screen uses guilt copy, a false countdown or difficulty tuned to force spend.
- [ ] The welcome-back screen shows what changed and what waits, and the comeback reward is smaller than active play earns.
- [ ] Every outbound rung has a holdout, and success is read on return-and-still-playing.
