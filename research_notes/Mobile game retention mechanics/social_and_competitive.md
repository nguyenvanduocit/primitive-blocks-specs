# Social, Competitive and Community Features in Mobile Games and Long-Term Retention

Research date: 2026-10-22. Method note: 19 web tool calls (15 searches, 4 page fetches). Search results were returned as summaries; pages I fetched directly are marked "(fetched)". Confidence labels: HIGH = primary or directly fetched source with a clear statement; MEDIUM = credible secondary source or search-summary of a named source; LOW = vendor, blog or unsourced claim. The headline finding is a negative one: no public, causal, guild-versus-solo D30/D90 number exists. Every multiplier I found is correlational, vendor-made or unsourced.

## 1. Which social features exist, and which measurably improve D30/D90 retention and LTV?

### Takeaway
Clans/guilds, friends, gifting, chat and co-op are the standard social set, and practitioners agree that connected players churn more slowly. I found no defensible published coefficient: the widely quoted "3x" and "4x" figures are unsourced, and academic evidence is mixed (one game showed an effect, one did not). The raw gap is probably inflated by self-selection (engaged players join guilds).

### Cited Findings
- Clans (guilds/teams) are groups of about 50 players who chat, share gifts and compete in challenges. Naavik says they raise retention through progression vectors (clan level), return triggers (daily donation limits, clan events) and social obligation (event progress, item donations). Published 2022-09-08, author Becky Matthew. Confidence: HIGH (fetched, but it is expert opinion, not measured data) — [Naavik, "Get Social: Building Effective Clan Features"](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/)
- The same Naavik article says clan-contribution live-ops (members work toward a shared goal) drive engagement and spending through limited-time events and fear of letting the clan down. It also says cosmetics and VIP system sales often rise alongside clan features because social players care about status. The article gives no LTV figures. Confidence: MEDIUM (opinion, no data) — [Naavik, 2022-09-08](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/)
- Naavik says Clash of Clans (released August 2012) earned a reported $296.75M in the year of the article, and calls clan features a strong driver of its ongoing success. Confidence: MEDIUM (reported figure, causal link asserted not shown) — [Naavik, 2022-09-08](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/)
- Naavik says Idle Mafia's higher LTV is attributed to its subscription system plus PvP and social features (its clan-like "Gangs"). No figures. Confidence: LOW — [Naavik, 2022-09-08](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/)
- Academic evidence is mixed. A master's thesis (University of Jyväskylä, Mikael Kitola, 2019) analysed analytics data from two commercial F2P mobile games using logistic regression. In Game 1, social-feature engagement in the first 7 days was linked to higher retention still visible at day 30. In Game 2, no such link was found. Days played was positively associated with retention in both. The author concludes social features do not guarantee better retention but a link holds "under certain conditions". Confidence: HIGH that this is what the thesis says (fetched); MEDIUM on generalisability (n=2 games, 2019, correlational) — [JYX record](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/66822)
- A 2017 MMORPG study found friend count, guild membership and guild role, combined with engagement and progression, improved lifetime prediction over benchmark models. Confidence: MEDIUM (search summary only; prediction, not causal uplift) — [Datalearner listing of the paper](https://datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0167-9236/volumes-and-issues/263/paper-detail/4446)
- An arXiv paper reportedly found achievement features matter from early to advanced stages while social features become the strongest longevity predictor only at the game's highest level. Confidence: LOW (search summary only, I did not open the paper; the search surfaced arXiv 1702.08005 as the likely match) — [arXiv 1702.08005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.08005.pdf)
- A practitioner blog (GameGrowthAdvisor, published 2026-03-17, updated 2026-08-01) states: "Players with at least one connection in the game churn noticeably slower in every title I have worked on, though I have never seen a defensible published coefficient for it." It lists "session-length and social-connection coefficients" as "widely quoted, sourced nowhere". It recommends an RPG guild hook for day 3-7 churn, social competition for casino/tabletop at day 1, and alliance mechanics for strategy at days 14-30, and calls this "judgement from 50+ launches", not measurement. Confidence: HIGH that this is what the source says (fetched); MEDIUM as evidence (single author's experience) — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)
- Quoted multipliers exist but are weak: one guide claims "at least one in-game friend by day 5" gives 3x higher D30 and that guild joiners are 4x more likely to keep playing; others claim 30-40% or 35% lifts. A research paper on PvP exposure was cited as showing raw retention gaps are confounded by severe self-selection. Confidence: LOW (blog/vendor claims, no methodology; I could not trace the original sources) — search results summarised from [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/mobile-game-retention), [Second Stage](https://secondstage.io/academy/player-lifecycle/lifecycle-metrics) and [arXiv 2603.16136](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16136); contradicted in spirit by the GameGrowthAdvisor statement above that no such coefficient is published.
- Discord claims a causal result for PC games: its "Social Layer" study reports a 31% increase in player retention, and (per a March 2026 industry blog citing Discord) PC players with a friend on Discord played a median of six times longer than those without, and eight times longer with three friends. Discord's Social Layer is now on iOS and Android. This is vendor-produced and covers PC, not mobile. Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM — [Outlook Respawn on Discord mobile tools](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/discord-takes-social-layer-to-mobile-with-new-gaming-tools)
- Pokemon GO: friendship levels rise through daily gifting and joint raids/gyms, and friendship unlocks bonuses in group battles. A GDC Summer 2020 recap says Niantic's internal data on social raiding informed decisions to pause features, and that players might take a "forever break" if social events stopped; no numbers published. A small Qriously survey (243 players) found playing with friends was the main reason people kept playing. Confidence: MEDIUM for mechanics, LOW for retention claims — [Massively OP GDC recap](https://massivelyop.com/?p=294874), [AndroidPolice, 2018-06-18](https://www.androidpolice.com/2018/06/18/two-years-pokemon-go-finally-gets-trading-ways-interact-friend-game/), [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-with-friends/)
- Royal Match (casual puzzle) has team features, but I found no source detailing how teams work or their retention effect. One comparison says Royal Match is less social-focused than Royal Kingdom (friends, leaderboards). A 2021 App Annie report put Royal Match D30 at 16.5%, the highest among match-3 titles examined, with in-game events and foreshadowed content common to the top five. Confidence: MEDIUM for the 2021 number (dated; PocketGamer.biz reporting); social effect not established — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/77656/dream-games-royal-match-leads-match-3-d30-retention-at-165/), [Esports.net comparison](https://www.esports.net/news/mobile-games/royal-match-vs-royal-kingdom/)

### Inferences
- The honest summary for a lay reader: "players with friends/guilds stay longer" is almost certainly true as a correlation, and plausibly partly causal through obligation and identity, but nobody outside the studios can quote a trustworthy multiplier. Treat any "4x" figure as marketing.
- Social features probably act differently by lifecycle stage: early friends/guild hook for D3-D7 (RPG), alliance for D14-D30+ (strategy), per the practitioner blog and the arXiv paper's "top-level" finding. This is consistent across LOW/MEDIUM sources but not independently verified.

### Gaps
- No causal (holdout/A-B) guild-membership D30/D90 or LTV uplift was found for any named game. Supercell, King, Dream Games publish none.
- No clan-specific retention figures for Clash of Clans / Clash Royale; only third-party tracker numbers (e.g., 18.4% D30 for Clash Royale, unmethodologized) which I did not use.
- Royal Match "Teams" mechanics and impact not found. Candy Crush social features not found.
- Friend-gifting ("sending lives") effects: no data found.
- No source for UGC (as in-game player-created content) retention in mobile specifically, other than Roblox (see section 4).

## 2. How competitive structures work (ladders, seasons, tiers, leaderboards, tournaments, matchmaking)

### Takeaway
Direct, citable evidence is thin: I found good research on matchmaking and churn, but little sourced material on how trophy ladders/seasons are tuned in specific mobile games. Matchmaking research shows that pure skill-matching is not retention-optimal, and that a loss streak is a churn trigger.

### Cited Findings
- EOMM (Engagement Optimized Matchmaking, WWW 2017, EA data): matches are chosen by predicted player churn rather than skill only. It improved the number of retained players over skill-based matchmaking by 0.3% to 1.1% across pool sizes of 200-500, with benefit accumulating over time. Confidence: MEDIUM (search summary of the paper; small effect; simulation on one game's data) — [arXiv 1702.06820](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.06820)
- A Management Science study on 5.4 million Lichess matches found matchmaking that accounts for players' reactions to recent wins/losses raised engagement 4-6% versus conventional skill-based matchmaking (SBMM); in theoretical scenarios gains reached up to 50%. A loss run can push players to stop queuing or quit for the night. Paid advantages can raise engagement under specific conditions. The article warns that opaque matchmaking could erode trust. Confidence: HIGH that this is what the article reports (fetched, article dated 2026-06-01; authors and study year not named in the article); MEDIUM on transfer to mobile (chess, not a mobile F2P game) — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/gaming-study-says-skill-based-matchmaking-is-fair-but-it-also-quietly-drives-players-away/)
- A Blizzard "Deprioritize Skill Test" (loosening skill constraints) decreased retention for 90% of the player base, as reported via a vendor-blog summary. Blizzard is an Activision Blizzard subsidiary; this is not a mobile test and I did not verify it against Blizzard's own publication. Confidence: LOW — [Acer blog](https://blog.acer.com/en/discussion/1867/what-is-sbmm-and-does-it-work-blizzard-sure-thinks-so)
- GameGrowthAdvisor (2026-03-17) recommends leaderboards "populated from cohorts rather than friends" and asynchronous challenges as solo-friendly competitive substitutes for small titles. Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM (opinion) — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)
- An unsourced statistics site claimed a 2020 Clash Royale season system cut mid-ladder drop-off by 15%. I rejected it as unreliable (no source cited). Confidence: LOW — [Sci-Tech-Today](https://www.sci-tech-today.com/stats/clash-royale-statistics/)

### Inferences
- The "win-rate tuning" players suspect (hidden systems nudging win/loss streaks) is an actual research direction (EOMM, loss-streak reaction studies), with small published gains of roughly 0.3-6%. I found no source confirming that any specific mobile game ships engagement-optimized matchmaking; do not state that as fact.
- Trophy/season resets exist to re-create a return trigger and keep the ladder fluid, but I have no cited source in my set; treat as MEDIUM from background knowledge of the genre, not from the notes' sources.

### Gaps
- No sourced description of trophy/tier/season mechanics, season-end rewards, or tournament formats for Clash Royale, Call of Duty Mobile, Marvel Snap, etc. A follow-up search on official patch notes or GDC talks is needed.
- No data on how much leaderboards or seasons lift D30/D90.
- Win-rate tuning in mobile (as opposed to PC/chess) is undocumented in my sources.

## 3. Social-obligation mechanics (co-op events, guild wars, team goals, sending lives, async play)

### Takeaway
Practitioners describe obligation (letting the team down) as the actual retention engine behind clans. Evidence is qualitative. The one structural fact with a source: alliances in 4X/survival games gate buffs, build help and event access, so leaving has a cost.

### Cited Findings
- Naavik: retention comes from "social obligation (event progress, item donations)" plus "return triggers (daily donation limits, clan events)". Shop Titans uses a guild-owned town members upgrade through donations, with contributions shown in an "investors" tab. Call of Duty: Warzone's "Happy Hour" gives double XP when playing with Regiment (clan) members. Confidence: HIGH that this is claimed (fetched); MEDIUM on effect size (none given) — [Naavik, 2022-09-08](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/)
- Fan guides for Whiteout Survival say alliances supply free build help, stat buffs and access to major events (the "largest power boost available"), and that leaving at the wrong time can cost event participation and rewards, with leadership handoff required for R5. Descriptive, not retention data. Confidence: MEDIUM for mechanic, LOW for any retention inference — [BlueStacks WoS alliance guide](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/white-out-survival/wos-alliance-guide-en.html), [Heaven Guardian guide](https://heaven-guardian.com/whiteout-survival-how-to-leave-alliance/)
- A StudioKrew post citing Sensor Tower Q3 2025 data says Whiteout Survival is regularly in the top 10 grossing survival titles with a 13% increase in sessions over a year, and credits alliance wars and PvP tournaments with extending longevity. The causal credit is commentary. Confidence: MEDIUM for Sensor Tower figure via secondary, LOW for causal claim — [StudioKrew](https://studiokrew.com/blog/?p=1466)
- Pokemon GO: friend gifting is limited to once per day per friendship and raising friendship needs joint activity, which creates a daily social return trigger. Confidence: MEDIUM — [AndroidPolice, 2018-06-18](https://www.androidpolice.com/2018/06/18/two-years-pokemon-go-finally-gets-trading-ways-interact-friend-game/)

### Inferences
- The design logic is consistent across sources: a shared goal plus a daily contribution limit turns a solo habit into a promise to other people. That is the likely reason these patterns look similar across games.
- Dark side (inference): obligation also produces churn when a player stops and the team is hurt; no source in my set quantifies this.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on "sending lives" (Candy Crush-style) or asynchronous play impact.
- No measured retention effect of guild wars or co-op events; only qualitative statements.

## 4. Community and creator ecosystem (Discord/Facebook, streamers, community events)

### Takeaway
Community channels and creator ecosystems are widely used, but quantified retention evidence comes mostly from the platforms selling them (Discord, Roblox). Roblox is the strongest case for UGC as a business model; its numbers are public.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox said developers earned over $1 billion through DevEx in the 12 months ending June 30, 2025; more than 100 developers earned over $1 million. Roblox raised the DevEx payout rate by 8.5% in September 2025, which management expects to weigh on 2026 margins. One report says DevEx fees were $423M in Q1 2026, up 50% year over year. Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH for the $1B figure (company statement via press), MEDIUM for Q1 2026 (single report) — [Nasdaq/Zacks on Roblox payouts](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/roblox-raises-creator-payouts-will-margins-face-pressure-2026), [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Roblox-continues-to-reward-game-content-creators-as-payouts-cross-1-billion.1157555.0.html), [Pocketgamer.biz DevEx rate rise](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/roblox-creators-will-now-earn-85-more-from-developer-exchange)
- A third-party site (RoLearn) estimates about $1.5B total creator payouts in 2025, $477M in Q4 2025. This differs from Roblox's $1B DevEx figure (different definitions are likely, unverified). Confidence: LOW — [RoLearn creator economy report](https://rolearn.dev/trend-reports/creator-economy-2025-report); contradicts/does not reconcile with Nasdaq above.
- Tencent Games' Delta Force Discord community reached about 800,000 members, with 8,000-10,000 daily joins at peak and 30-50% retention (retention definition not given), using Discord Quests and thematic branding. This is a vendor-hosted case study. Confidence: LOW — [casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/discord/case-study/tencent-games-grows-discord-community-to-800k-members-with-30-50-retention)
- A Discord-focused blog notes game Discord channels are busy for a few weeks after launch and then fade to announcements only. Discord's developer guide says detailed engagement/retention data unlocks at 500 members. Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM — [Playio blog](https://blog.playio.co/discord-marketing-game-studios), [Discord developer docs](https://docs.discord.com/developers/game-development/how-to-create-a-community-for-your-game)
- Royal Match runs a Facebook group where players share progress and ask for help (single mention). Confidence: LOW — [Esports.net](https://www.esports.net/news/mobile-games/royal-match-vs-royal-kingdom/)
- AppsFlyer's 2024 gaming report, as cited by GameGrowthAdvisor, found D30 retention peaked at 7.5% when user-generated content was used in creatives (an acquisition finding, not in-game UGC). Confidence: LOW (second-hand, I did not open the AppsFlyer report) — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)

### Inferences
- Community channels likely matter most as an off-game return path and a support channel, but I found no mobile-specific causal data. The "fade after launch" note suggests Discord needs live-ops events to stay active.

### Gaps
- Streamer/creator-code programs (e.g., Supercell Creator Code, Fortnite Support-A-Creator) and their effect on installs or retention: no sources found.
- No independent (non-vendor) data on Discord/Facebook group effects on mobile retention.
- Community-event cadence (e.g., esports, "Brawl Stars championship") not covered.

## 5. Viral and referral loops (invite rewards, share-to-unlock, realistic K-factor)

### Takeaway
K-factor = invites per user x invite conversion rate. Sustained K above 1 is rare; a realistic goal is 0.3-0.5 that lowers blended acquisition cost. Examples of K>1 in games are unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Formula: K = i x c (invites sent per user times conversion rate). One guide says most healthy waitlists land at K = 0.3-0.7; another says a realistic goal for most products is K of 0.3-0.5 that meaningfully lowers blended acquisition cost. Even below 1, higher K lowers effective CPI. Confidence: MEDIUM (multiple marketing blogs agree; no primary data) — [Udonis K-factor](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/k-factor), [LaunchList guide](https://blog.getlaunchlist.com/blog/viral-coefficient-k-factor-guide), [Echai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/retention-referral/what-s-a-viral-coefficient-and-is-chasing-a-k-factor-above-1-realistic-for-my-kind-of-product)
- K>1 is generally limited to products where inviting others is core to the value (messaging, collaboration, marketplaces). Confidence: MEDIUM — [Echai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/retention-referral/what-s-a-viral-coefficient-and-is-chasing-a-k-factor-above-1-realistic-for-my-kind-of-product)
- A forum answer cites WhatsApp, Among Us and Wordle as K>1 examples with no data; I treat these as unverified. Confidence: LOW — [LaunchRock forum](https://www.launchrock.com/community/questions/1830/are-there-any-examples-of-games-or-apps-that-have-a-k-factor-viral-coefficient)
- Incentive caveat: sources advise raising invite incentives, but one warns the coefficient often drops when incentives are removed. Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM — [Kurve](https://kurve.co.uk/blog/app-referral-marketing-k-factor-viral-retention)
- Niantic's Pokemon GO raids drew in-person groups ("hundreds of players gathering"), an anecdote of organic social spread, not a K measurement. Confidence: LOW — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-with-friends/)

### Inferences
- For a typical mobile game, invite rewards are best thought of as a modest CPI discount (K roughly 0.1-0.5) rather than a growth engine. The lower bound of 0.1 is my own estimate; no source gives a game-specific range.

### Gaps
- No game-specific K-factor data (Clash, Roblox, Among Us measured). All K figures come from generic app/marketing blogs.
- Share-to-unlock effectiveness not found.
- Reddit/TikTok organic virality was out of scope (UA researcher).

## 6. Which genres depend most on social, and which can skip it?

### Takeaway
Sources agree in direction: 4X/strategy and MMO-RPG lean hardest on alliances/guilds; casual puzzle uses lighter team layers; small titles often cannot support social features because they lack player density. Evidence is practitioner judgement, not measurement.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner guidance by genre: RPGs should invest first in a guild hook (churn at days 3-7), casino/tabletop in social competition (day 1), strategy in alliance mechanics (days 14-30). The author calls it "judgement from 50+ launches". Confidence: MEDIUM (single expert, explicitly non-measured) — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)
- Last War: Survival Game is described as having a social ecosystem driven by player-led alliances; Wikipedia reports monthly revenue rising from $30M (January 2024) to $138M (December 2024). Wikipedia is secondary and I could not verify the underlying source. Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM — [Wikipedia: Last War: Survival Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_War:_Survival_Game)
- Whiteout Survival: see section 3 (Sensor Tower Q3 2025 via StudioKrew). Confidence: MEDIUM — [StudioKrew](https://studiokrew.com/blog/?p=1466)
- Casual puzzle (Royal Match) is described as less social-centric than its sibling Royal Kingdom, while still featuring teams. Confidence: LOW — [Esports.net](https://www.esports.net/news/mobile-games/royal-match-vs-royal-kingdom/)
- Naavik says clans need networked play and treats clans and live-ops as late-game features, and that without supporting features (clan level, events, chat) the expected KPI impact is negligible. Confidence: MEDIUM (opinion) — [Naavik, 2022-09-08](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/)

### Inferences
- Games with long competitive power curves (4X, MMO, gacha RPG) are the ones where a group provides real in-game advantage, so social becomes structural. Hyper-casual and single-player puzzle can usually skip it, but I have no direct source stating that.

### Gaps
- Naavik's Last War analysis was referenced in search but I could not retrieve it; no sourced figures for alliance-driven retention in Last War, Rise of Kingdoms or Whiteout Survival.
- No data on PvP shooters (Call of Duty Mobile) social/clan retention effects.
- Contradiction to flag: no source contradicts the genre ranking, but the ranking itself is not backed by cohort data.

## 7. Costs and risks (moderation, toxicity, cheating, cold start)

### Takeaway
Social features add server cost, moderation burden and a cold-start problem. Toxicity has survey-measured spending and playtime effects, though the best numbers come from vendors or non-mobile contexts.

### Cited Findings
- Clans are costly and complex to build, raise concurrent users and server costs, may need another funding round, and AI bots can fake validation but are not sustainable at scale. Naavik advises building clans in small, validated increments. The article does not discuss moderation. Confidence: HIGH that this is stated (fetched); MEDIUM as evidence (opinion) — [Naavik, 2022-09-08](https://naavik.co/f2p-mobile/building-clan-features/)
- "Toxicity and the Bottom Line" (2024; sample 2,408 active gamers): 19% of players spend less money and 21% reduce playtime to avoid harassment; 44% disable voice chat, 31% abandon active matches, 27% leave lobbies to avoid abuse. Confidence: MEDIUM (search summary; the report was published by a moderation-industry party, I did not open it) — [Game Industry Library](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/toxicity-and-the-bottom-line-2024), [GameDeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/toxicity-the-bottom-line-5-benefits-of-toxmod-voice-moderation)
- ToxMod (Modulate) blog claims 35% of players exposed to toxicity leave the game and that voice moderation costs much more than text. Vendor claim. Confidence: LOW — [GameDeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/toxicity-the-bottom-line-5-benefits-of-toxmod-voice-moderation)
- ADL/UC Irvine research (Dr. Steinkuehler) examined hate speech in games and player retention among 600 players aged 13-25; I did not retrieve the retention numbers. Confidence: LOW for effect size — [ADL "The Price of Hate in Online Games"](https://www.adl.org/resources/article/price-hate-online-games)
- Cold start and bots: Warzone Mobile players reported a bot-balancing update filled lobbies with low-skill bots to ease newcomers, with some saying they were bored after one or two games; a Steam commenter claims many mobile games use AI copies of player accounts in ranked. Anecdotal. Confidence: LOW — [Insider Gaming on Warzone Mobile](https://insider-gaming.com/warzone-mobile-bot-balancing/), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2767030/discussions/0/596260472619015559)
- Small-title advice: most social features assume a player density that small titles lack; alternatives are asynchronous challenges, shared community milestones and cohort-populated leaderboards. Confidence: MEDIUM (opinion) — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)
- Opaque matchmaking can erode player trust (Management Science / Digital Trends item in section 2). Confidence: MEDIUM — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/gaming-study-says-skill-based-matchmaking-is-fair-but-it-also-quietly-drives-players-away/)

### Inferences
- A small game should start with asynchronous social (shared goals, cohort leaderboards, ghost opponents) before real-time chat or clans, because it avoids both the cold-start and moderation costs. This follows from the Naavik and GameGrowthAdvisor sources but is my synthesis.

### Gaps
- No mobile-specific toxicity data, no cheating/botting data (e.g., effect of cheaters on mobile PvP retention), no moderation cost per MAU figures.
- No source quantified the cold-start threshold (minimum players needed for a clan or a ladder to work).
- The Unity 2023 survey and ADL 2022 report cited by vendors were not retrieved.

## Cross-cutting notes for the report writer
- Strongest evidence quality in this set: Naavik (2022, expert), the Jyväskylä thesis (2019, 2 games), the Lichess/EOMM matchmaking studies (not mobile), Roblox payout numbers. Everything else is weak.
- Most important caveat to carry into the report: "players with friends or guilds retain far better" has no trustworthy published multiplier, and self-selection probably accounts for part of the gap. The thesis result (one of two games showed an effect) is the best hard evidence available here.
- Most data is dated 2017-2022 for the social-retention link; the only 2026 sources (GameGrowthAdvisor, Digital Trends, Roblox earnings, Discord) are either blogs, news coverage or vendor material.
- Tool coverage: 19 tool calls; Mobile Dev Memo, Deconstructor of Fun, GameRefinery, AppsFlyer/Adjust reports and GDC talks (suggested sources) did not surface in searches and were not read directly.
